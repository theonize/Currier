# 2 Kings 4:1-7 - Widow's oil multiplied — Study Handout

## Verses

> **1** Now a certain woman of the wives of the sons of the prophets cried out to Elisha, saying, “Your servant my husband is dead. You know that your servant feared Yahweh. Now the creditor has come to take for himself my two children to be slaves.”
>
> **2** Elisha said to her, “What should I do for you? Tell me: what do you have in the house?” She said, “Your servant has nothing in the house, except a pot of oil.”
>
> **3** Then he said, “Go, borrow empty containers from all your neighbors. Don’t borrow just a few containers.
>
> **4** Go in and shut the door on you and on your sons, and pour oil into all those containers; and set aside those which are full.”
>
> **5** So she went from him, and shut the door on herself and on her sons. They brought the containers to her, and she poured oil.
>
> **6** When the containers were full, she said to her son, “Bring me another container.” He said to her, “There isn’t another container.” Then the oil stopped flowing.
>
> **7** Then she came and told the man of God. He said, “Go, sell the oil, and pay your debt; and you and your sons live on the rest.”
>
> — *World English Bible (WEB)*

---

## Overview

This happens in Israel about 850 years before Jesus. The king worshiped Baal, and the prophets who stayed loyal to God were poor. One of them has died and left his family in debt. Back then, if you couldn't pay, the lender was allowed to take your kids as workers until the debt was paid off.

So a mom with nothing left goes to Elisha, God's prophet. He doesn't hand her money. He asks what she has, gives her strange instructions, and sends her and her two sons to work. Behind a shut door, one small flask of oil fills every jar they can find.

**Big idea:** God hears the cry of people in trouble and keeps his promise to them. He starts with the little they have and turns it into enough to pay what they owe and live.

## Key Verse

> **2** Elisha said to her, “What should I do for you? Tell me: what do you have in the house?” She said, “Your servant has nothing in the house, except a pot of oil.”

---

## Main Points

1. **Cry for Help** (v.1) — A prophet's widow tells Elisha plainly that her God-fearing husband is dead and a creditor is coming to take her two sons as slaves.
2. **What's in the House?** (v.2) — Elisha asks what she has, and she names one small flask of oil.
3. **Borrow, Shut, Pour** (vv.3-6) — She and her sons gather many jars, shut the door, and pour. The oil stops only when there are no more jars.
4. **Sell, Pay, Live** (v.7) — The man of God sends her to sell the oil. The debt is paid and the family lives on the rest.

---

## Info

### 1. Cry for Help (v.1)

#### v.1
- "Cried out" is the word for asking someone with power to step in. God's law promises that when a widow cries out, "I will surely hear their cry" (Exodus 22:23).
- Her husband "feared Yahweh," and he still died poor. Loving God doesn't mean nothing bad ever happens.
- She doesn't hide her problem. She says it straight to the person who can help.

### 2. What's in the House? (v.2)

#### v.2
- "What should I do for you?" is the same question Elijah once asked Elisha (2 Kings 2:9). Now Elisha passes it on.
- Elisha asks what she *has*, not just what she *doesn't* have.
- "Nothing... **except**." The thing she almost doesn't count is the thing God uses.
- The "pot" was a tiny flask, the kind you'd keep for your skin or hair. It wasn't worth much.

### 3. Borrow, Shut, Pour (vv.3-6)

#### v.3
- She has to knock on every neighbor's door and ask. Everyone will see she's broke.
- "Don't borrow just a few." Elisha doesn't give her a number. How many jars is up to her.

#### v.4
- "Shut the door." This isn't a show. Elisha isn't even there.
- Her sons are in the room. They're part of it.

#### v.5
- She does exactly what he said, before a single drop of extra oil appears.
- The boys keep bringing jars, and she keeps pouring. The same kids who were about to be taken away are now carrying the jars that save them.

#### v.6
- One of the sons says the line that ends it: "There isn't another container."
- The oil didn't run out. It *stopped*. God's supply wasn't the limit; the jars were.

### 4. Sell, Pay, Live (v.7)

#### v.7
- She goes back and checks in with Elisha before doing anything with the oil.
- The order matters: sell, **pay** what you owe, then live on what's left. God's gift doesn't skip honesty.
- The family isn't rich now. They have enough. The sons who were about to be taken **live**.

## Discussion

1. Read v.1 again. List everything that has gone wrong for this family. What is she about to lose?
2. Elisha could have just fixed the problem. Why do you think he asks, "What do you have in the house?" first?
3. The oil stopped when the jars ran out (v.6). What was the limit: God's supply, or something else? What does that say about God?
4. The widow didn't hide her problem, and her sons didn't sit it out; they carried the jars. When you or your family hit a hard spot, who do you go to? And where could you be one of the ones carrying jars or lending them?
