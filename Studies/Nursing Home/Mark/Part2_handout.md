# Mark 1:9-13 - Baptism and temptation — Study Handout

## Verses

> **9** In those days, Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee, and was baptized by John in the Jordan.
>
> **10** Immediately coming up from the water, he saw the heavens parting, and the Spirit descending on him like a dove.
>
> **11** A voice came out of the sky, “You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”
>
> **12** Immediately the Spirit drove him out into the wilderness.
>
> **13** He was there in the wilderness forty days tempted by Satan. He was with the wild animals; and the angels were serving him.
>
> — *World English Bible (WEB)*

---

## Overview

John the Baptist has just promised that someone "mightier" than he is coming, one who will baptize with the Holy Spirit (Mark 1:7-8). Now that one arrives. Around AD 27-29, Jesus walks from the small village of Nazareth down to the Jordan River. There he is baptized alongside crowds who are confessing their sins.

Mark tells the story at a run. In five short verses heaven is torn open, the Spirit comes down, and the Father speaks. Then, without a pause, the Spirit sends Jesus into the wilderness for forty days of testing. Jesus says nothing in the whole scene. Heaven names him before he preaches, heals, or calls a single disciple. So the reader learns who Jesus is before anyone else in the story does.

**Big idea:** At the Jordan God tears open the heavens to name Jesus his beloved, Spirit-anointed Son, and the same Spirit at once drives him into the wilderness to be tested by Satan, so that the Son's identity is declared before any work and proved in conflict.

## Key Verse

> **11** A voice came out of the sky, “You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

---

## Main Points

1. **The Son with sinners** (v.9) — Jesus comes from obscure Nazareth and, without explanation, is baptized by John among people confessing their sins.
2. **Heaven torn, Son named** (vv.10-11) — "Immediately" the heavens are torn, the Spirit descends like a dove, and the Father declares him beloved before any public ministry.
3. **The Son driven and tested** (vv.12-13) — "Immediately" the Spirit drives him into the wilderness, where Satan tests him forty days among wild animals while angels serve him, with no outcome narrated.

---

## Info

### 1. The Son with sinners (v.9)

#### v.9
- **"Jesus came"** answers John's promise: "after me comes he who is mightier" (1:7). The promised one arrives in the very next sentence.
- **Nazareth** was a village of only a few hundred people. The Old Testament never mentions it (compare John 1:46).
- **baptized** (Greek: βαπτίζω, *baptizō*) means "immerse" or "dip." John's baptism was a sign of repentance (1:4-5).
- **Why was Jesus baptized?** Mark gives no reason. Jesus is not confessing sin. He is standing where sinners stand (2 Corinthians 5:21).

### 2. Heaven torn, Son named (vv.10-11)

#### v.10
- **"Immediately"** is Mark's signature word, and this is its first use. The story moves fast.
- **"parting"** is literally **torn** (Greek: σχίζω, *schizō*), the word for ripping cloth. It answers Isaiah's prayer: "Oh that you would tear the heavens, that you would come down" (Isaiah 64:1).
- **Torn again:** Mark uses this word only once more. At Jesus' death the temple curtain is torn (15:38).
- **The Spirit** that John promised (1:8) now comes down on Jesus (compare Isaiah 42:1). The dove was a humble bird, the offering of the poor.

#### v.11
- **The only words spoken** in the passage are spoken *to* Jesus: "You are."
- **Echoes of Scripture:**
  - the royal son of Psalm 2:7;
  - the Servant of Isaiah 42:1, in whom God delights;
  - possibly Isaac, the "beloved son" (Genesis 22:2).
- **beloved** (Greek: ἀγαπητός, *agapētos*) also stands for "only son" in the Greek Old Testament. Mark uses it again at 9:7 and 12:6.
- **Love comes first:** the Father's delight comes *before* any recorded deed. Jesus doesn't earn it.

### 3. The Son driven and tested (vv.12-13)

#### v.12
- **"Immediately"** again: there is no gap between the word of love and the wilderness.
- **drove out** (Greek: ἐκβάλλω, *ekballō*) is a strong word. Mark uses the same verb for casting out demons.
- **Not led into comfort:** being led by the Spirit didn't mean being led into comfort.

#### v.13
- **Forty days** recalls Israel's forty years of testing in the wilderness (Deuteronomy 8:2). Where Israel failed, the Son goes in.
- **tempted** (Greek: πειράζω, *peirazō*) can mean "test" or "tempt," and the testing lasts all forty days. God tempts no one (James 1:13).
- **The wild animals** may mean danger, or peace with creation (Isaiah 11:6-9). Either way, the Son is safe in God's keeping.
- **The angels were serving him,** as an angel once fed Elijah (1 Kings 19:5-8). The same verb returns in Mark 10:45: the Son "came not to be served, but to serve."

## Discussion

1. In these five verses, what does Jesus himself do, and what is done to him or said over him?
2. Why might Mark place the Father's "You are my beloved Son" *immediately* before the Spirit drives Jesus into the wilderness?
3. Jesus had no sin, yet he stepped into a river where people were confessing their sins. What does that tell us about him?
4. Have you known a hard season that came right after a real blessing? Where did you see God's care for you there?
