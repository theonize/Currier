# Mark 1:14-20 - Jesus begins ministry and calls disciples — Study Handout

## Verses

> **14** Now after John was taken into custody, Jesus came into Galilee, preaching the Good News of God’s Kingdom,
>
> **15** and saying, “The time is fulfilled, and God’s Kingdom is at hand! Repent, and believe in the Good News.”
>
> **16** Passing along by the sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and Andrew the brother of Simon casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen.
>
> **17** Jesus said to them, “Come after me, and I will make you into fishers for men.”
>
> **18** Immediately they left their nets, and followed him.
>
> **19** Going on a little further from there, he saw James the son of Zebedee, and John, his brother, who were also in the boat mending the nets.
>
> **20** Immediately he called them, and they left their father, Zebedee, in the boat with the hired servants, and went after him.
>
> — *World English Bible (WEB)*

---

## Overview

John the Baptist has been arrested. Around AD 27-29, Jesus comes into Galilee, the land ruled by Herod Antipas, the very man who had John locked up. There Jesus begins to preach. His message fits in one breath: God's appointed time has come, and God's reign has drawn near, so turn around and trust this good news.

Then, walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus sees two pairs of brothers at work. Simon and Andrew are casting a net. James and John are mending nets in their father's boat. Jesus calls, and they come. They leave their nets, the boat, and even their father to follow him. The fishermen don't say a word. Their answer is what they do. Mark gives no reason except the one who called them, and his promise: "I will make you into fishers for men."

**Big idea:** At the appointed time Jesus announces that God's reign has drawn near and summons all to repent and believe, then calls four fishermen by name, who leave nets and father to follow him.

## Key Verse

> **15** and saying, “The time is fulfilled, and God’s Kingdom is at hand! Repent, and believe in the Good News.”

---

## Main Points

1. **The good news announced** (vv.14-15) — After John is handed over, Jesus comes into Galilee announcing that God's appointed time is full and his reign has drawn near, and he calls everyone to repent and believe.
2. **Called from the nets** (vv.16-18) — Jesus sees Simon and Andrew at work and says, "Come after me, and I will make you into fishers for men"; at once they leave their nets and follow.
3. **Called from the father's boat** (vv.19-20) — Jesus calls James and John at once, and the cost grows: they leave their father, the boat and the hired servants to go after him.

---

## Info

### 1. The good news announced (vv.14-15)

#### v.14
- **"taken into custody"** is literally **handed over** (Greek: παραδίδωμι, *paradidōmi*). Mark later uses the same word for Jesus (9:31; 15:15) and for his followers (13:9). God's messengers pay a price.
- **Galilee** belonged to Herod Antipas, the ruler who had just arrested John. Jesus starts preaching right there.
- **Good News** (Greek: εὐαγγέλιον, *euangelion*) echoes Isaiah: "How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news... who says to Zion, 'Your God reigns!'" (Isaiah 52:7).

#### v.15
- **First the news, then the call.** "The time is fulfilled" and "God's Kingdom is at hand" come before "Repent" and "believe." What God has done comes first.
- **fulfilled** pictures a measure filled to the brim. God's appointed time has fully come (Galatians 4:4).
- **Kingdom** (Greek: βασιλεία, *basileia*) means God's reign, God acting as King, more than a place.
- **Repent** (Greek: μετανοέω, *metanoeō*) is the prophets' call to turn back to God with your whole life (Zechariah 1:3). **Believe in** means resting your whole weight on the good news. The two go together (Acts 20:21).

### 2. Called from the nets (vv.16-18)

#### v.16
- **"he saw":** Jesus takes the first step. A later Jewish saying told students, "Make for yourself a teacher." Here the teacher does the choosing.
- **Simon, later called Peter,** is named twice. He is the first disciple named in Mark and the last (16:7).
- **casting a net** means throwing a round, weighted net, done by one man from the shore or in shallow water.

#### v.17
- **"Come after me"** was a Hebrew way of saying "give me your allegiance." Israel was told to "walk after" the LORD (Deuteronomy 13:4).
- **"I will make you"** is a promise. Jesus does the making, and it takes time.
- **"fishers for men"** is a play on their trade. Jesus takes the work they know and points it toward people.

#### v.18
- **"Immediately":** Elisha asked to kiss his parents goodbye before following Elijah (1 Kings 19:20). These men ask nothing.
- **left** (Greek: ἀφίημι, *aphiēmi*) means "let go." The nets were their living.
- **Looking ahead:** these same men would one day leave Jesus and run (14:50). Yet after the resurrection he sent word to "his disciples and Peter" (16:7).

### 3. Called from the father's boat (vv.19-20)

#### v.19
- **The same pattern again:** Jesus moves on, sees two brothers at work, and calls.
- **Two Johns:** the passage begins with one John taken away (v.14) and ends with another John called (v.19).
- **mending** (Greek: καταρτίζω, *katartizō*) means putting the nets back in working order, probably after a night's fishing.

#### v.20
- **The cost grows:** first nets, now nets, boat and father. Grown sons normally stayed and took up their father's trade.
- **hired servants** show a real family business. With them in the boat, Zebedee was likely not left helpless.
- **"went after him"** answers Jesus' "Come after me" (v.17) with the same word "after."
- **Not everyone leaves home.** Jesus sent one man home to "tell them what great things the Lord has done for you" (5:19). Every follower gives Jesus first place.

## Discussion

1. In vv.16-20, what does Jesus do, and what do the four fishermen say or do?
2. In v.15 Jesus announces what God has done before he says what we must do. Why does that order matter?
3. Jesus says, "I will make you into fishers for men." Why is it good news that he does the making?
4. Jesus met these men on an ordinary workday and pointed their lives toward other people. Where you are now, who is one person you could pray for by name, or tell what the Lord has done for you?
