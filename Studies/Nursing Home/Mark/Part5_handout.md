# Mark 1:29-34 - Healings at Simon's house — Study Handout

## Verses

> **29** Immediately, when they had come out of the synagogue, they came into the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John.
>
> **30** Now Simon’s wife’s mother lay sick with a fever, and immediately they told him about her.
>
> **31** He came and took her by the hand, and raised her up. The fever left her, and she served them.
>
> **32** At evening, when the sun had set, they brought to him all who were sick, and those who were possessed by demons.
>
> **33** All the city was gathered together at the door.
>
> **34** He healed many who were sick with various diseases, and cast out many demons. He didn’t allow the demons to speak, because they knew him.
>
> — *World English Bible (WEB)*

---

## Overview

It is still the same Sabbath in Capernaum. Jesus has just taught in the synagogue and driven out an unclean spirit (1:21-28). Now he walks into the home of Simon and Andrew, with James and John, the four men he has just called (1:16-20). Simon's mother-in-law is in bed with a fever. They tell Jesus about her. He takes her by the hand and raises her up, and she serves them.

At sunset the Sabbath ends, and the whole town brings its sick and demon-possessed to the door. Jesus heals many and casts out many demons. The demons know him, but he will not let them speak. This story does not promise that every fever will break tonight. It shows a Lord who comes near and raises up.

**Big idea:** On one Sabbath in Capernaum, the authority Jesus showed in the synagogue enters a fisherman's house and fills its doorway: with a wordless grasp of the hand he raises a fevered woman to serve, heals and delivers the town's afflicted at sunset, and silences the demons who know him.

---

## Key Verse

> **31** He came and took her by the hand, and raised her up. The fever left her, and she served them.

---

## Main Points

1. **The hand that raises** (vv.29-31) — Jesus comes to a bedridden woman, takes her hand and raises her, and her serving shows the cure is complete.
2. **The town at the door** (vv.32-34a) — When the sun sets and the Sabbath closes, the town brings its sick and those possessed by demons to the door, and Jesus heals many and casts out many demons.
3. **The silenced witnesses** (v.34b) — Jesus will not let the demons speak "because they knew him," for knowing about him is not faith, and he will not be announced on their terms.

## Info

<div class="info-columns">

### 1. The hand that raises (vv.29-31)

#### v.29
- **Same day:** Jesus' authority enters a home.
- **The first house in Mark** belongs to two of the first disciples called.

#### v.30
- **fever** (Greek: πυρέσσω, *pyressō*) means "to burn with fever." Without modern medicine it could be dangerous. Mark never calls it a punishment.
- **"they told him":** this is the first request in Mark made for someone else. She doesn't speak. Others bring her need to Jesus, plainly and at once.

#### v.31
- **He came.** Mark records no word from Jesus, only a hand. He comes close and touches her.
- **raised** (Greek: ἐγείρω, *egeirō*) is an ordinary word for helping someone up. Mark uses it again at the empty tomb (16:6). The echo is suggestive, but should not be pressed.
- **served** (Greek: διακονέω, *diakoneō*) shows the cure was complete. She is the first human in Mark to serve, the word Jesus uses for his own mission (10:45). It doesn't pay for her healing. It is a grateful response.

### 2. The town at the door (vv.32-34a)

#### v.32
- **"when the sun had set":** sunset ended the Sabbath. Mark doesn't say why they waited. Most likely they were keeping the ban on carrying burdens (Jeremiah 17:21-22).
- **"they brought"** pictures a steady stream. Many of the sick couldn't come alone. Neighbors carried them.
- **Two groups:** Mark keeps the sick and the demon-possessed apart. Not every illness is demonic.

#### v.33
- **"All the city"** is an overstatement. Capernaum was a village. Picture perhaps a few hundred people filling a narrow lane.
- **A crowd is not yet disciples.** Next morning Jesus slips away to pray, then leaves for other towns (1:35-38).

#### v.34a
- **various** (Greek: ποικίλος, *poikilos*) means "many-colored." Healing diseases is the work Scripture gives to God (Psalm 103:3).
- **"many"** stresses how much he did, not who was left out. Matthew 8:16 and Luke 4:40 say he healed them all.
- **Not a guarantee.** These healings were signs of God's kingdom. Faithful believers still stayed sick (2 Timothy 4:20). Full healing waits for the resurrection (Romans 8:23).

### 3. The silenced witnesses (v.34b)

#### v.34b
- **"they knew him":** the demons had the facts, but no allegiance. "The demons also believe, and shudder" (James 2:19).
- **Why silence them?** Jesus won't let hostile witnesses define him. He is rightly known through the cross and resurrection (9:9). The first human in Mark to call him "Son of God" watches him die (15:39).
- **Who is silenced?** The intruding voice, not the person. Jesus gives people back to themselves (5:15).

## Discussion

1. In vv.30-32, who brings the sick to Jesus, and what does Jesus do for Simon's mother-in-law?
2. The woman was raised and served. The demons knew Jesus and were silenced. What is the difference between knowing about Jesus and following him?
3. This passage does not promise that every fever will break tonight. What does it show about Jesus that we can trust while we wait?
4. The woman in bed did not have to speak. Others told Jesus about her. Whose name can you bring to Jesus today, and whose hand could you take?
