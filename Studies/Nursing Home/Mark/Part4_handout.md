# Mark 1:21-28 - Authority over demons — Study Handout

## Verses

> **21** They went into Capernaum, and immediately on the Sabbath day he entered into the synagogue and taught.
>
> **22** They were astonished at his teaching, for he taught them as having authority, and not as the scribes.
>
> **23** Immediately there was in their synagogue a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out,
>
> **24** saying, “Ha! What do we have to do with you, Jesus, you Nazarene? Have you come to destroy us? I know you who you are: the Holy One of God!”
>
> **25** Jesus rebuked him, saying, “Be quiet, and come out of him!”
>
> **26** The unclean spirit, convulsing him and crying with a loud voice, came out of him.
>

> **28** The report of him went out immediately everywhere into all the region of Galilee and its surrounding area.

> — *World English Bible (WEB)*

---

## Overview

The four fishermen have just left their nets to follow Jesus. Now they go with him into Capernaum, a small village on the Sea of Galilee. It is the Sabbath, around AD 27-29, and the people have gathered in the synagogue to hear Scripture. This is the first public act of Jesus that Mark describes.

Jesus teaches, and the people are astonished. Then a man with an unclean spirit cries out. The spirit knows who Jesus is, and it is afraid. Jesus uses no object, no formula, and no higher name. He gives two short commands, and the spirit leaves. The crowd calls it a new teaching, and the news spreads across Galilee. Mark's readers know what the crowd does not yet know: this is the Son of God (1:1).

**Big idea:** In Jesus' first public act, the authority of his teaching proves itself in a bare command that expels an unclean spirit: God's holy and sovereign authority has arrived in person, and the Kingdom Jesus announced has begun its victory over evil.

## Key Verse

> **25** Jesus rebuked him, saying, “Be quiet, and come out of him!”

---

## Main Points

1. **Teaching with authority** (vv.21-22) — On the Sabbath Jesus teaches in the Capernaum synagogue, and his hearers are astonished, for he does not teach as the scribes do.
2. **The spirit's outcry** (vv.23-24) — An unclean spirit protests, fears destruction, and names Jesus "the Holy One of God."
3. **The word that expels** (vv.25-26) — Jesus silences the spirit with two commands, and it comes out, convulsing the man.
4. **Verdict and report** (vv.27-28) — The crowd calls this a new teaching that even unclean spirits obey, and the report spreads through Galilee.

## Info

<div class="info-columns">

### 1. Teaching with authority (vv.21-22)

#### v.21
- **"They went"** implies the four fishermen came too, to watch Jesus at work.
- **synagogue** (Greek: συναγωγή, *synagōgē*) means a gathering. People met on the Sabbath to hear the Law read and explained.
- **"taught":** Mark doesn't tell us what Jesus said, only how it struck them.

#### v.22
- **authority** (Greek: ἐξουσία, *exousia*) means rightful authority. The word brackets the story (vv.22, 27).
- **scribes** were trained experts in the Law. Their authority was real but borrowed. Jesus spoke as one whose word was itself final.

### 2. The spirit's outcry (vv.23-24)

#### v.23
- **"in their synagogue":** evil was there in the place of worship. Jesus' presence exposed it.
- **unclean spirit** echoes God's promise to remove "the spirit of impurity" (Zechariah 13:2).
- **Not every illness.** Mark tells the sick apart from those with demons (1:32-34). This story gives no reason to assume illness of body or mind is demonic.

#### v.24
- **"What do we have to do with you"** is an old protest: "Why are you interfering with us?"
- **destroy:** later Jesus' enemies plot to "destroy" him with this same word (3:6; 11:18).
- **"the Holy One of God"** means one wholly set apart to God, the opposite of unclean. The spirit has the facts but no trust (James 2:19).

### 3. The word that expels (vv.25-26)

#### v.25
- **rebuked** (Greek: ἐπιτιμάω, *epitimaō*) is used of God, who "rebuked the Red Sea" (Psalm 106:9). The archangel Michael said, "May the Lord rebuke you!" (Jude 9). Jesus simply rebukes.
- **"Be quiet"** is literally "be muzzled." He says the same to the storm (4:39).
- **Only a word.** Other exorcists used roots, rings and incantations. In Greek Jesus' command is five words.

#### v.26
- **convulsing him:** the spirit's last act is violence. But Jesus rebuked the spirit, not the man. The man is set free.
- **"came out of him"** repeats Jesus' words (v.25). His command is obeyed exactly.

### 4. Verdict and report (vv.27-28)

#### v.27
- **amazed:** wonder is not yet faith. Mark never says they repented or believed (1:15).
- **new** (Greek: καινός, *kainos*) tends to mean new in kind, not just the latest. The crowd calls a deed a teaching: his word and his work are one.
- **"they obey him":** the disciples later say this of wind and sea and ask, "Who then is this?" (4:41). "What?" becomes "Who?"

#### v.28
- **report** (Greek: ἀκοή, *akoē*) is literally "what is heard." It is still reputation, not faith.
- **"went out"** repeats the verb of vv.25-26. The spirit goes out of the man, and the news goes out into Galilee.

## Discussion

1. In vv.21-28, what does Jesus do, and how do the spirit and the people respond?
2. The spirit says true words about Jesus, yet Jesus tells it, "Be quiet" (vv.24-25). Why is knowing the right words about Jesus not enough?
3. The crowd was amazed, but Mark never says they believed (vv.27-28). What is the difference between being amazed at Jesus and following him?
4. Jesus is stronger than whatever holds us. Where you are now, what is one fear or burden you could bring to him in prayer?
