# Mark 1:21-28 - Authority over demons — Study Handout

## Verses

> **21** They went into Capernaum, and immediately on the Sabbath day he entered into the synagogue and taught.
>
> **22** They were astonished at his teaching, for he taught them as having authority, and not as the scribes.
>
> **23** Immediately there was in their synagogue a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out,
>
> **24** saying, “Ha! What do we have to do with you, Jesus, you Nazarene? Have you come to destroy us? I know you who you are: the Holy One of God!”
>
> **25** Jesus rebuked him, saying, “Be quiet, and come out of him!”
>
> **26** The unclean spirit, convulsing him and crying with a loud voice, came out of him.
>
> **27** They were all amazed, so that they questioned among themselves, saying, “What is this? A new teaching? For with authority he commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey him!”
>
> **28** The report of him went out immediately everywhere into all the region of Galilee and its surrounding area.
>
> — *World English Bible (WEB)*

---

## Overview

On a Sabbath around AD 28, Jesus and the four fishermen he has just called walk into Capernaum, Peter's hometown on the Sea of Galilee. Jesus goes into the synagogue, where the village gathered each week to hear Scripture read and explained, and he teaches. The people are astonished. The scribes leaned on interpretation handed down to them. Jesus teaches as one who has authority himself.

Then a man with an unclean spirit cries out. The spirit knows who Jesus is and fears him. Jesus answers with two short commands, and the spirit leaves. He uses no object, no ritual and no one else's name. Mark keeps sickness and evil spirits distinct (1:34) and places no blame on the man. This is Jesus' first public act in Mark, and the news spreads all over Galilee.

**Big idea:** In Jesus' first public act, the authority of his teaching proves itself in a bare command that expels an unclean spirit: God's holy and sovereign authority has arrived in person, and the Kingdom Jesus announced has begun its victory over evil.

## Key Verse

> **25** Jesus rebuked him, saying, “Be quiet, and come out of him!”

---

## Main Points

1. **Teaching with authority** (vv.21-22) — On the Sabbath Jesus teaches in the Capernaum synagogue, and the people are astonished: he teaches with authority, not as the scribes.
2. **The spirit's outcry** (vv.23-24) — An unclean spirit cries out, fears Jesus has come to destroy, and names him "the Holy One of God."
3. **The word that expels** (vv.25-26) — Jesus silences the spirit with two short commands, and it comes out, convulsing the man.
4. **Verdict and report** (vv.27-28) — The amazed crowd calls this "a new teaching" that even unclean spirits obey, and the news spreads through Galilee.

---

## Info

### 1. Teaching with authority (vv.21-22)

#### v.21
- **"They went":** the four fishermen came too. Their first lesson in following was watching his authority at work.
- **The synagogue** could invite a visitor to speak (Luke 4:16-21). That is how Jesus, with no office, came to teach.

#### v.22
- **astonished** (Greek: ἐκπλήσσω, *ekplēssō*) means "struck out" of one's composure, "dumbstruck."
- **The scribes** were experts in the Law. Their authority was borrowed, resting on the text and on teaching handed down. Mark doesn't mock them (12:34).
- **authority** (Greek: ἐξουσία, *exousia*) means the right and power to act. Jesus spoke as one whose word was itself final.

### 2. The spirit's outcry (vv.23-24)

#### v.23
- **"In their synagogue":** the trouble was already there, in the holy assembly. Jesus' presence brought it into the open.
- **unclean spirit** means an evil spirit, unfit for God's presence. God had promised to remove "the spirit of impurity" from the land (Zechariah 13:2).
- **Not an illness.** Mark tells the two apart: "He healed many who were sick with various diseases, and cast out many demons" (1:34). Nothing here blames the man; Mark passes no judgment on him.

#### v.24
- **"What do we have to do with you"** is an old Hebrew protest: "Why are you interfering with us?"
- **"Have you come to destroy us?"** Many Jews expected God's reign to end evil spirits' power. This one seems to fear its time is up.
- **"the Holy One of God"** is true. It means one wholly set apart to God, the opposite of "unclean." Yet it comes from an enemy: "The demons also believe, and shudder" (James 2:19).

### 3. The word that expels (vv.25-26)

#### v.25
- **rebuked** is the Greek Old Testament's word for God's rebuke of hostile powers: he "rebuked the Red Sea also, and it was dried up" (Psalm 106:9).
- **No borrowed name.** An angel said, "Yahweh rebuke you, Satan!" (Zechariah 3:2). Jesus simply rebukes, in his own name.
- **"Be quiet"** literally means "be muzzled." Jesus later says the same word to the stormy sea (4:39).
- **No props.** Other exorcists used rings, roots, smoke or spells. Jesus only speaks.

#### v.26
- **convulsing** means tearing or pulling to and fro. The spirit's last act is violence, but it obeys.
- **"came out of him"** answers Jesus' command: the spirit does what he says.
- **The man remains, free.** Jesus rebukes the spirit, not the man. Compare a later freed man, "sitting, clothed, and in his right mind" (5:15).

### 4. Verdict and report (vv.27-28)

#### v.27
- **"They were all amazed."** But amazement is not yet faith. Mark never says they repented or believed (1:15).
- **"A new teaching?"** The crowd calls a command a teaching: Jesus' word and deed are one. Of God, Scripture says, "He spoke, and it was done" (Psalm 33:9).
- **"What is this?"** The spirit knew who Jesus is; the crowd saw only what he did. Later the disciples ask, "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?" (4:41).

#### v.28
- **report** literally means "what is heard." The spirit went out of the man; now the news goes out through Galilee.
- **A pattern begins.** Jesus goes through Galilee "preaching and casting out demons" (1:39).
- **Fame is not faith.** Fame alone can even hinder Jesus' work (1:45).

## Discussion

1. In vv.21-26, what does Jesus say and do, and what does he not use?
2. The spirit knows exactly who Jesus is (v.24), while the crowd asks, "What is this?" (v.27). Why is knowing about Jesus not the same as trusting him?
3. The crowd calls a command to an unclean spirit "a new teaching" (v.27). What does that tell us about Jesus' words?
4. Jesus' word carries his own authority, and even unclean spirits obey it. When you hear Scripture read this week, how could you listen to it as his word to you, not just as good advice?
