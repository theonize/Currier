# Leader's Notes — James 3:13-18 - Wisdom from Above

## Verses

> **13** Who is wise and understanding among you? By his good conduct let him show his works in the meekness of wisdom.
>
> **14** But if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your hearts, do not boast and be false to the truth.
>
> **15** This is not the wisdom that comes down from above, but is earthly, unspiritual, demonic.
>
> **16** For where jealousy and selfish ambition exist, there will be disorder and every vile practice.
>
> **17** But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial and sincere.
>
> **18** And a harvest of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace.
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

- **Setting** — Jewish believers in Jesus scattered across the Dispersion (James 1:1), meeting in synagogue-style assemblies where would-be teachers competed for honor. An honor-shame world where the title "wise man" was a contested prize. Early date, c. AD 45-49, possibly the earliest New Testament writing.
- **Author** — James, half-brother of Jesus and head of the Jerusalem church (Acts 15:13-21), remembered as "James the Just" — a man whose own byname matches the "harvest of righteousness" he commends. Martyred c. AD 62 (Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.200).
- **Immediate context** — the climax of chapter 3. Having warned "not many of you should become teachers" (3:1) and anatomized the tongue (3:2-12), James now names the disease behind the symptom: which *wisdom* is driving the mouth? The passage also pivots into the quarrels of 4:1-12.
- **Genre and frame** — wisdom exhortation in the ancient "two-ways" pattern (Psalm 1; Proverbs 9; Deuteronomy 30): two wisdoms set side by side and judged by their fruit. Opens with a diatribe-style challenge question (v.13), closes with a farmer's proverb (v.18).
- **Structure** — thesis question (v.13) → wisdom from below: root, origin, fruit (vv.14-16) → wisdom from above: origin, eightfold character (v.17) → the harvest (v.18). The keyword **from above** (*anōthen*) ties back to 1:17 — "every good gift... is from above."
- **Big idea** — true wisdom comes down from God and proves itself in meek, peaceable conduct; the counterfeit — clever, ambitious, combative — is exposed by the disorder it breeds, while peacemakers reap a harvest of righteousness.
- **Supporting movements**:
  - Wisdom is *shown*, not claimed — by good conduct in meekness (v.13).
  - False wisdom is traced downward: earthly → unspiritual → demonic (vv.14-16).
  - True wisdom is profiled in eight marks, "first pure" (v.17).
  - Peace is both the seed and the soil of righteousness (v.18).

---

## Topics

- **Two wisdoms** — every life runs on a wisdom from above or from below, and each is known by its fruit (vv.13-18).
- **Wisdom shown in meekness** — the proof of wisdom is conduct and controlled strength, not cleverness or winning arguments (v.13).
- **Jealousy and selfish ambition** — the twin heart-vices behind community conflict, breeding disorder and every vile practice (vv.14-16).
- **The peacemaker's harvest** — righteousness grows like a crop, sown in peace by those who make peace (vv.17-18).

---

## Info

### v.13
- "Who is wise and **understanding**?" — **epistēmōn** (Greek: ἐπιστήμων, *epistēmōn*, G1990), a New Testament hapax; the pair echoes Deuteronomy 1:13, Moses' search for "wise and understanding" leaders — this is a leadership question, following 3:1.
- "Let him **show**" (Greek: δειξάτω, *deixatō*) — wisdom is demonstrated, not claimed; mirrors 2:18, "show me your faith... I will show you my faith by my works."
- "Good **conduct**" (Greek: ἀναστροφή, *anastrophē*, G391) — a settled manner of life, a track record, not a performance.
- "**Meekness** of wisdom" (Greek: πραΰτης, *praÿtēs*, G4240) — not weakness but controlled strength; the virtue of Moses (Numbers 12:3) and of Jesus, "gentle and lowly in heart" (Matthew 11:29).
- Cultural inversion: in an honor culture, James relocates true honor in the very posture that culture read as weakness.

### v.14
- "**Bitter** jealousy" (Greek: ζῆλος πικρός, *zēlos pikros*, G2205) — *pikros* is the word for the "bitter" water of 3:11; James has found the polluted spring: the jealous heart.
- "**Selfish ambition**" (Greek: ἐριθεία, *eritheia*, G2052) — from *erithos*, "hired laborer"; in Aristotle, the mercenary canvassing of partisans for office. Faction-spirit, the campaigner's vice — precisely the temptation of those rushing to the teacher's seat.
- Located "in your **hearts**" — the disease is inward before it is ever spoken.
- "Do not boast and be false to the truth" — present imperatives: *stop* the ongoing boasting; self-promotion by the envious is itself a lie against the truth.

### v.15
- "Not the wisdom that **comes down from above**" — *anōthen* picks up 1:17: every good gift descends from the Father of lights; wisdom is asked for and received (1:5), never self-generated.
- The descending triad, in stark asyndeton: "**earthly** (Greek: ἐπίγειος, *epigeios*, G1919), **unspiritual** (Greek: ψυχικός, *psychikos*, G5591), **demonic** (Greek: δαιμονιώδης, *daimoniōdēs*, G1141)" — from earth, to the merely natural soul, to hell.
- *Psychikos* — "soulish": the cleverness native to human nature without the Spirit (cf. 1 Corinthians 2:14; Jude 19).
- *Daimoniōdēs* — a NT hapax, "demon-like." Counterfeit wisdom is not a neutral mistake; it is energized from below — same source as the tongue "set on fire by hell" (3:6).

### v.16
- A verbless proverb: "where jealousy and selfish ambition, there disorder and every vile practice" — compressed like a maxim.
- "**Disorder**" (Greek: ἀκαταστασία, *akatastasia*, G181) — political word for instability, even anarchy; cognate with the "restless" tongue (3:8) and the "unstable" double-minded man (1:8). The letter's signature vice: instability.
- The vice-pair repeats from v.14, bracketing the false-wisdom panel — one twin root, one inevitable harvest.
- Anticipates 4:1-2: "What causes quarrels and fights among you?"

### v.17
- "**First pure**" (Greek: ἁγνός, *hagnos*, G53) — *prōton* marks logical priority, not list order: purity is the root; the other seven qualities grow from it.
- The seven that follow: **peaceable** (εἰρηνικός), **gentle** (ἐπιεικής — yielding, not insisting on full rights), **open to reason** (εὐπειθής — teachable; a hapax), **full of mercy and good fruits**, **impartial** (ἀδιάκριτος — undivided; a hapax), **sincere** (ἀνυπόκριτος — without a mask).
- The Greek list is crafted for the ear — three *e*-initial virtues chime; the last two rhyme as alpha-privatives ("un-divided / un-masked") — order in the words enacting the order this wisdom brings.
- "Full of mercy" reverses 3:8 — the tongue "full of deadly poison"; *adiakritos* answers both the wavering doubter (1:6) and the partial judges (2:4).
- Closest parallels: the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23) and the Beatitudes (Matthew 5:5-9) — a portrait that finally looks like Christ himself.

### v.18
- "A **harvest of righteousness** is **sown in peace**" — agrarian proverb; the seed sown quietly determines the crop reaped later (Proverbs 11:18; Hosea 10:12; Isaiah 32:17 — "the effect of righteousness will be peace").
- "By those who **make peace**" — the Greek dative allows both "sown *by*" and "sown *for*" peacemakers: they labor at peace and they reap its produce.
- One Greek word away from Jesus' beatitude: "Blessed are the **peacemakers**" (Matthew 5:9).
- The verse answers the opening question: the truly wise are recognizable — they are the peace-sowers.

## Discussion

1. Look at v.13. How does James say real wisdom is proven — and what surprises you about the word "meekness"?
   - **Leader's note:** Draw out the contrast between claiming wisdom and showing it by conduct. Meekness is controlled strength (Moses, Numbers 12:3; Jesus, Matthew 11:29), not weakness — the opposite of needing to win every argument.
2. In v.15 James calls jealousy-driven cleverness "earthly, unspiritual, demonic." Why such strong language for attitudes we often treat as normal?
   - **Leader's note:** Trace the descending staircase: what isn't from God's Spirit is merely natural at best, and at root shares hell's character — the same source as the tongue's fire (3:6). Envy and rivalry are never neutral; v.16 shows they always harvest disorder.
3. Read the eight marks of v.17 slowly. Which one do you find hardest — and what would it look like in an ordinary week?
   - **Leader's note:** Let each person pick one mark honestly. Key: "first pure" — the other seven grow from purity of motive. Note the family resemblance to the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23) and to Jesus himself: this wisdom is asked for and given (1:5), not manufactured.
4. Verse 18 pictures peace as seed sown now and righteousness as the harvest later. Where is God asking you to sow peace this week — and what makes that sowing costly?
   - **Leader's note:** Keep it concrete: a relationship, a conversation, a grudge released. Peacemaking is active reconciliation, not conflict-avoidance — and the farmer's image means the harvest is slow but sure; peacemakers reap what they sow (Matthew 5:9; Galatians 6:7-9).
