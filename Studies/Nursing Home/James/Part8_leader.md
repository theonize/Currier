# Leader's Notes — James 4:1-12 - Warning Against Worldliness

## Verses

> **1** What causes quarrels and what causes fights among you? Is it not this, that your passions are at war within you?
>
> **2** You desire and do not have, so you murder. You covet and cannot obtain, so you fight and quarrel. You do not have, because you do not ask.
>
> **3** You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, to spend it on your passions.
>
> **4** You adulterous people! Do you not know that friendship with the world is enmity with God? Therefore whoever wishes to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God.
>
> **5** Or do you suppose it is to no purpose that the Scripture says, "He yearns jealously over the spirit that he has made to dwell in us"?
>
> **6** But he gives more grace. Therefore it says, "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble."
>
> **7** Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.
>
> **8** Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded.
>
> **9** Be wretched and mourn and weep. Let your laughter be turned to mourning and your joy to gloom.
>
> **10** Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you.
>
> **11** Do not speak evil against one another, brothers. The one who speaks against a brother or judges his brother, speaks evil against the law and judges the law. But if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law but a judge.
>
> **12** There is only one lawgiver and judge, he who is able to save and to destroy. But who are you to judge your neighbor?
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

- **Setting** — Jewish believers in Jesus scattered across the Dispersion (James 1:1), meeting in synagogue-style assemblies (James 2:2). Socially mixed congregations where the scramble for honor, property, and status had curdled into open faction. Early date, c. AD 45-49: Judea seething with banditry and proto-Zealot unrest, a famine under Claudius (c. AD 46-48) sharpening the fight over limited goods — "wars and battles" were not dead metaphors to these hearers.
- **Author** — James, half-brother of Jesus and head of the Jerusalem church, remembered as "James the Just," a man of prayer with "knees worn hard as a camel's." Here he writes as a **prophet conducting a covenant lawsuit** — "You adulterous people!" is Hosea's idiom, not a lecturer's — and as the peacemaker who presided over the Acts 15 settlement. Martyred c. AD 62 (Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.200).
- **Immediate context** — the turn from analysis to altar call. The "jealousy and selfish ambition" that breed "disorder" (3:14-16) now erupt as "quarrels and fights" (v.1). Threads from chapter 1 close here: the **double-minded** man of 1:8 is summoned by name (v.8); the promise "ask God" (1:5-6) is inverted into "you do not ask... you ask wrongly" (vv.2-3); "the lowly brother... exalted" (1:9) becomes "humble yourselves... he will exalt you" (v.10).
- **Genre and frame** — moral exhortation (paraenesis) in the diatribe manner: rhetorical questions, a stinging vocative, an imagined objector, a volley of commands. Beneath the Greek surface runs the prophetic **rîb** (covenant lawsuit): accusation of the unfaithful bride, summons to return, promise of restoration.
- **Structure** — four movements on a down-then-up arc: **symptom** (conflict, vv.1-3) → **diagnosis** (adultery with the world, vv.4-6) → **cure** (ten imperatives of repentance, vv.7-10) → **test case** (the judging tongue, vv.11-12). The hinge is v.6, "But he gives more grace" — the indicative that grounds every command that follows.
- **Big idea** — our quarrels grow from cravings that have quietly become gods and from a heart flirting with the world; the cure is not winning the argument but humbling ourselves before a jealous, gracious God who draws near to those who draw near — and stepping down from the judgment seat that is his alone.
- **Supporting movements**:
  - Conflict is traced inward: passions "at war in your members" (vv.1-3).
  - Worldliness is not weakness but adultery against a jealous Husband (vv.4-5).
  - Grace comes "to the humble" — so the way up runs down (vv.6-10).
  - Slander usurps the bench of the one Lawgiver and Judge (vv.11-12).

---

## Topics

- **The war within** — quarrels among believers spring from unsatisfied cravings inside them, and from prayer that is absent or self-serving (vv.1-3).
- **Friendship with the world** — courting the world's values is spiritual adultery against a God who yearns jealously over his people (vv.4-5).
- **Grace to the humble** — God opposes the proud but gives greater grace to those who submit, draw near, and grieve their sin; the way up is down (vv.6-10).
- **One Lawgiver and Judge** — to slander or judge a brother is to sit on a bench that belongs to God alone (vv.11-12).

## Info

### v.1
- Diatribe opening: doubled "**From where**?" (Greek: πόθεν, *pothen*) presses for a cause; James answers his own question with a second — the hearer cannot escape the verdict.
- "**Quarrels**... **fights**" (Greek: πόλεμοι καὶ μάχαι, *polemoi kai machai*, G4171/G3163) — literally "wars and battles," the words for armed strife and civic faction; Greco-Roman philosophy (Plato, *Phaedo* 66c) also traced war to bodily desire.
- "**Passions**" (Greek: ἡδοναί, *hēdonai*, G2237) — root of "hedonism"; not neutral pleasure but craving as a *power*.
- "**At war**" (Greek: στρατευομένων, *strateuomenōn*, G4754) — present participle: the cravings are troops permanently on campaign "in your members." The outer battle is the overflow of an inner mutiny.
- Cross-refs: Romans 7:23 ("law... waging war in my members"); 1 Peter 2:11; James 1:14-15 (desire conceives sin).

### v.2
- Six staccato verbs with almost no connectives; the Greek is unpunctuated, so translations choose between three couplets (KJV) and cause-and-effect (ESV/NIV: "desire... *so* you murder").
- "**You murder**" (Greek: φονεύετε, *phoneuete*, G5407) — unanimous in the manuscripts; Erasmus's guess "you envy" has no support. Best read as prophetic hyperbole in the manner of Jesus (Matthew 5:21-22) and 1 John 3:15 — yet chillingly literal in a homeland sliding toward bloodshed (cf. James 5:6).
- "**Covet**" (Greek: ζηλοῦτε, *zēloute*, G2206) — the chain desire → murder, covet → fight runs along the tenth and sixth commandments (Exodus 20:13, 17), internalizing the law as Jesus did.
- "**Because you do not ask**" — the failure of prayer; reverses James 1:5-6 ("let him ask God"). Acquisition is rerouted from rivalry with neighbors to dependence on a giving Father (1:17).

### v.3
- "You **ask wrongly**" (Greek: κακῶς αἰτεῖσθε, *kakōs aiteisthe*, G2560/G154) — the fault is motive, not technique.
- Purpose clause: "to **spend**" (Greek: δαπανήσητε, *dapanēsēte*, G1159 — squander, consume) "on your passions." Prayer as a procurement service for the *hēdonai*.
- The catchword **passions** brackets vv.1-3: what fuels the fight also corrupts the prayer.
- Cross-ref: 1 John 5:14 (asking according to his will).

### v.4
- "**You adulterous people!**" (Greek: μοιχαλίδες, *moichalides*, G3428) — feminine, "adulteresses": the whole congregation is cast as YHWH's unfaithful **bride** (Hosea 1-3; Jeremiah 3:20; Ezekiel 16, 23). Later scribes added "adulterers and" (KJV) to blunt the deliberate feminine.
- "**Friendship**" (Greek: φιλία, *philia*, G5373) — NT hapax; in the ancient world a binding alliance of shared loyalties and enemies (Aristotle; Cicero, *De Amicitia*), not casual fondness. Contrast Abraham, "friend of God" (James 2:23).
- "**The world**" (Greek: κόσμος, *kosmos*, G2889) — not creation but the value-system organized against God.
- "**Enmity**" (Greek: ἔχθρα, *echthra*, G2189) — "whoever *resolves* to be" (aorist subjunctive: a settled choice of will) "**makes himself**" (Greek: καθίσταται, *kathistatai*) an enemy — the result follows from the alliance's own nature.
- Cross-refs: Matthew 6:24 (two masters); 1 John 2:15-17; Romans 8:7.

### v.5
- The hardest sentence in the letter. "The Scripture says" — but no OT verse matches; James likely distills the theme of the **jealous God** (Exodus 20:5; 34:14, "whose name is Jealous"; Deuteronomy 32:16-21; Zechariah 8:2).
- Three grammatical knots: who "**yearns**" (Greek: ἐπιποθεῖ, *epipothei*, G1971 — love-sick longing)? Is "the spirit" subject or object? Is "**jealously**" (Greek: πρὸς φθόνον, *pros phthonon*, G5355 — normally malignant *envy*) negative or positive?
- ESV/NASB: God yearns jealously over the spirit **he made to dwell** (Greek: κατῴκισεν, *katōkisen* — the better-attested transitive reading favors God as subject). KJV: "the spirit... lusteth to envy."
- Point either way: divine jealousy is not petty insecurity but the exclusive love of a faithful Husband who will not share his bride — the perfect counter to v.4's adultery. Creation echo: Genesis 2:7, the breath God gave and owns.

### v.6
- "But he gives **more grace**" (Greek: μείζονα χάριν, *meizona charin*, G5485) — the gospel hinge; the adversative pivots from indictment to invitation. Grace *greater* than the world's pull and the war within.
- Quotation of **Proverbs 3:34 LXX**; 1 Peter 5:5 cites the identical form (both read "God" for the LXX's "Lord") — a fixed early-Christian catechism text on humility.
- "**Opposes**" (Greek: ἀντιτάσσεται, *antitassetai*, G498) — military: God "draws up battle lines against" the proud. The war of v.1 now has God on the field.
- "**Proud**" (Greek: ὑπερήφανος, *hyperēphanos*, G5244) — literally "showing oneself above"; "**humble**" (Greek: ταπεινός, *tapeinos*, G5011) — lowly. Heaven's posture toward a person answers that person's posture.
- Humility is the empty hand grace requires, not a work that earns it (Isaiah 57:15; 66:2).

### v.7
- First of **ten aorist imperatives** (vv.7-10) — decisive, urgent acts; the densest command-cluster in the letter.
- "**Submit**" (Greek: ὑποτάγητε, *hypotagēte*, G5293) — passive with reflexive force, "fall into rank under"; same military root as v.6's "opposes." The proud find God arrayed against them; the humble fall in under God.
- "**Resist** the devil" (Greek: ἀντίστητε τῷ διαβόλῳ, *antistēte tō diabolō*, G436/G1228) — *diabolos* = "the slanderer" (pointed, given v.11). Only appearance of Satan by title in James; he stands behind the "demonic" wisdom of 3:15.
- Order matters: submission to God precedes and empowers resistance (Ephesians 6:10-13; 1 Peter 5:8-9; Matthew 4:1-11).
- Command + promise formula begins: "and he **will flee**" (cf. *Testament of Naphtali* 8:4).

### v.8
- "**Draw near** to God, and he will draw near to you" (Greek: ἐγγίσατε... ἐγγιεῖ, *engisate... engiei*, G1448) — cultic language for approaching the presence, now open to every penitent. The structural **pivot** of vv.7-10. Echoes Zechariah 1:3; Malachi 3:7 ("Return to me, and I will return to you"); Psalm 145:18; Hebrews 10:22.
- "**Cleanse** your hands... **purify** your hearts" (Greek: καθαρίσατε... ἁγνίσατε, *katharisate... hagnisate*, G2511/G48) — priestly washing (Exodus 30:19-21) fused with Psalm 24:4 "clean hands and a pure heart" and Isaiah 1:16. Hands = deeds; heart = motive.
- Two addresses, two faults: "you **sinners**" (hands) and "you **double-minded**" (hearts). **Double-minded** (Greek: δίψυχοι, *dipsychoi*, G1374) — "two-souled," James's own coinage, verbatim from 1:8: the man torn between two friendships (v.4) is the man with two souls. Cure: Psalm 86:11, "unite my heart."

### v.9
- Liturgy of repentance, not private regret: "**Be wretched** and **mourn** and **weep**" (Greek: ταλαιπωρήσατε, πενθήσατε, κλαύσατε; G5003/G3996/G2799) — the Joel 2:12-13 summons to lament in Greek dress.
- Four NT hapaxes in one verse — *be wretched*, **laughter** (γέλως), **be turned** (μετατρέπω), **gloom** (κατήφεια, *katēpheia*, G2726: the downcast face) — James heightens his diction at the emotional climax.
- Inverts the world's mirth: Luke 6:25 ("Woe to you who laugh now"); answers Matthew 5:4 ("Blessed are those who mourn"); Ecclesiastes 7:2-4.
- Not a prescription for a joyless faith (James 1:2 commands joy) — the point is grief that refuses to laugh off sin (2 Corinthians 7:10).

### v.10
- "**Humble yourselves**" (Greek: ταπεινώθητε, *tapeinōthēte*, G5013) — passive form: "let yourselves be lowered"; picks up "the humble" of v.6 and closes the frame opened by "submit" (v.7).
- "He will **exalt** you" (Greek: ὑψώσει, *hypsōsei*, G5312) — the lifting is wholly God's. The way up runs down.
- The great reversal of Jesus: Matthew 23:12; Luke 14:11; 18:14; and of Hannah and Mary (1 Samuel 2:7-8; Luke 1:52). Christ walked this path first: Philippians 2:8-9.
- Sovereignty and responsibility held together: we submit, cleanse, humble; God gives grace, draws near, exalts (cf. Philippians 2:12-13).

### v.11
- The test case: the un-humbled man caught in the act. Tone softens to "**brothers**" (Greek: ἀδελφοί, *adelphoi*, G80) — the rebuke was the severity of love.
- "**Speak evil against**" (Greek: καταλαλεῖτε, *katalaleite*, G2635) — slander, defamation; sounded three times; "judge" (κρίνω, G2919) six times across vv.11-12 — the act turned back on the actor by sheer repetition. Torah forbade it: Leviticus 19:16.
- Logic: to slander a brother is to judge *him*; to judge him is to judge the **law** (νόμος ×4) — likely the "royal law" of neighbor-love (James 2:8; Leviticus 19:18) — and so to set oneself above its Author.
- "Not a **doer** of the law but a judge" — recalls 1:22-25. The critic has resigned from obedience to take up a bench.
- Cross-refs: Matthew 7:1-5; Romans 14:4, 10-13; James 3:9 (cursing image-bearers).

### v.12
- "There is only **one**" (Greek: εἷς, *heis*) — emphatic; the Shema's "one" (Deuteronomy 6:4; James 2:19). Isaiah 33:22: "the LORD is our judge, our lawgiver, our king" — three offices in one Person.
- "**Lawgiver**" (Greek: νομοθέτης, *nomothetēs*, G3550) — NT hapax; the revered founder-legislator of a Greek city (Solon, Lycurgus), reserved here for God alone.
- "Able to **save** and to **destroy**" (Greek: σῶσαι καὶ ἀπολέσαι, G4982/G622) — Deuteronomy 32:39 ("I kill and I make alive"); Matthew 10:28. Final judgment is God's alone, now exercised through Christ (John 5:22; Acts 17:31).
- "But **who are you**?" (Greek: σὺ δὲ τίς εἶ, *sy de tis ei*) — the creaturely put-down of Romans 9:20; 14:4. "**Neighbor**" (Greek: πλησίον, *plēsion*, G4139; a variant reads "the other") echoes Leviticus 19:18 — the slanderer judges the very neighbor he is commanded to love.
- Thread runs on to James 5:9: "the Judge is standing at the door."

## Discussion

1. Read vv.1-3. Where does James say our quarrels really come from — and what does he say has gone wrong with our praying?
   - **Leader's note:** Push past "other people" to the inward source: passions at war *within* (v.1). The chain is desire → lack → fight. Then the two prayer failures: not asking at all (v.2), and asking to fund our cravings (v.3). Tie to 1:5 — God gives generously to those who ask rightly.
2. James calls friendship with the world "adultery" (v.4) and says God "yearns jealously" over us (v.5). Why marriage language — and is God's jealousy good news or bad news?
   - **Leader's note:** Surface the prophets' picture of Israel as God's bride (Hosea). Friendship in that world was a sworn alliance, so "friend of the world" means a rival lover. God's jealousy is the exclusive love of a faithful husband who will not share us — bad news for divided loyalty, good news for anyone who wants to be wanted. Then v.6: "But he gives more grace."
3. Look at the ten commands in vv.7-10 and the promises attached to them ("he will flee," "he will draw near," "he will exalt you"). Which of these feels most urgent for you right now?
   - **Leader's note:** Let people pick one honestly. Show the frame: submit (v.7) and humble yourselves (v.10) bracket the whole; the center is "draw near... he will draw near" (v.8). Resistance flows from surrender, not willpower. Don't skip v.9 — real repentance may feel like grief before it feels like joy — but note James is not commanding a gloomy faith (1:2).
4. Verses 11-12 say that when we speak against or judge a fellow believer we are sitting in God's seat. Where are you most tempted to do that — and what would stepping down look like this week?
   - **Leader's note:** Keep it concrete and gentle: "venting," "concern," "just being honest" (Application §2). Key text: "one lawgiver and judge... who are you?" (v.12). Stepping down = taking it to God or to the person, not to an audience; returning to being a *doer* of the law of neighbor-love (2:8). Close on grace: the same God who opposes the proud exalts the humble.
