# James 4:13-17 - Boasting About Tomorrow — Study Handout

## Verses

> **13** Come now, you who say, "Today or tomorrow we will go into such and such a town and spend a year there and trade and make a profit"—
>
> **14** yet you do not know what tomorrow will bring. What is your life? For you are a mist that appears for a little time and then vanishes.
>
> **15** Instead you ought to say, "If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that."
>
> **16** As it is, you boast in your arrogance. All such boasting is evil.
>
> **17** So whoever knows the right thing to do and fails to do it, for him it is sin.
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

James has just rebuked the believer who climbs onto God's judgment seat (4:11-12). Now he turns to another overreach: the traveling merchant who plans a whole year as though tomorrow were his property. The Roman peace had made the Mediterranean one great marketplace, and these traders spoke as the business world taught them — city, duration, activity, profit, all in the confident future tense, with no mention of God. James does not condemn planning or profit. He quotes the merchant's own words, then answers with two facts the man had edited out: he cannot see one day ahead, and his life is a mist. The cure is not to stop planning but to say, and mean, "If the Lord wills." Then James widens the lens into a proverb: to know the good and not do it is sin.

**Big idea:** God alone holds tomorrow and our life is a vapour, so every plan must be held under "if the Lord wills" — and once we know that, failing to live it is sin.

---

## Key Verse

> **15** Instead you ought to say, "If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that."

---

## Main Points

1. **The Plan Without God** (v.13) — James quotes a confident year-long business plan whose only flaw is that God is nowhere in it.
2. **You Are a Mist** (v.14) — The planner who schedules a year cannot see one day ahead, because his own life is a vapour that appears and vanishes.
3. **If the Lord Wills** (vv.15-16) — The same plans, spoken under God's will, are right; spoken as self-confident boasting, they are evil.
4. **The Good Left Undone** (v.17) — Knowing the right thing and failing to do it is sin — the sin of omission, and the letter's "doers, not hearers" made into a rule.

---

## Info

### 1. The Plan Without God (v.13)

#### v.13
- **"Come now"** (Greek: ἄγε νῦν, *age nyn*) — a prophet's buttonhole, used in the New Testament only here and in 5:1.
- Four confident futures: **go, spend a year, trade, make a profit**. Place, time, activity, and outcome all settled in advance. Not one clause for God.
- **"Trade"** (Greek: ἐμπορεύομαι, *emporeuomai*) is the traveling wholesale merchant, root of "emporium." **"Profit"** shares its root with "gain the whole world and forfeit his soul" (Mark 8:36).
- Seed text: Proverbs 27:1, "Do not boast about tomorrow." Nearest story: Jesus' rich fool (Luke 12:16-21).

### 2. You Are a Mist (v.14)

#### v.14
- The man who schedules a **year** cannot guarantee a **day**. That gap is the measure of his folly.
- **"Mist"** (Greek: ἀτμίς, *atmis*) — steam off a pot, breath on a cold morning; the morning mist of Hosea 13:3 and Ecclesiastes' "vapour." In Greek it "appears" and then "un-appears" — the same word with a negative on the front.
- The best manuscripts read "**you** are a mist," not "it is a vapour." James is describing the planners themselves.
- "My life is a breath" (Job 7:7); "all flesh is grass" (Isaiah 40:6-8). Not despair but clarity: "teach us to number our days" (Psalm 90:12).

### 3. If the Lord Wills (vv.15-16)

#### v.15
- James scripts the right speech. The cure is a corrected sentence, not silence.
- **"If the Lord wills"** (Greek: ἐὰν ὁ κύριος θελήσῃ, *ean ho kyrios thelēsē*) — the old *Deo volente*, "D.V." Paul used it habitually (Acts 18:21; 1 Corinthians 4:19).
- Notice what survives: "we will live and do this or that." The plans remain; they move under one condition, and **life itself** comes first. Cf. Proverbs 16:9; Luke 22:42.

#### v.16
- **"Arrogance"** (Greek: ἀλαζονεία, *alazoneia*) — the swagger of the braggart who claims what he does not have; here, ownership of tomorrow (cf. "the pride of life," 1 John 2:16).
- Announcing plans and gains was normal status display then. James calls it **evil**, not merely unwise, because it takes what belongs to God alone.
- "Lord willing" recited while the heart still grips the plan is the very arrogance James condemns.

### 4. The Good Left Undone (v.17)

#### v.17
- **"So"** — the specific rebuke becomes a universal rule, a line fit for Proverbs.
- The **sin of omission**: sin defined not by breaking a rule but by leaving a known good undone. **"Sin"** (Greek: ἁμαρτία, *hamartia*) means "missing the mark."
- Knowledge creates obligation: the servant who knew his master's will (Luke 12:47); the goats condemned for what they did *not* do (Matthew 25:45).
- The verb **"do"** runs through the paragraph — "spend [make] a year" (v.13), "do this or that" (v.15), "fails to do it" (v.17). So much busy doing, and the one needful doing left undone.
- Within James: "be doers of the word, and not hearers only" (1:22). It also forbids the opposite error: hearing "life is a mist" and ceasing to act.

## Discussion

1. Read the merchant's speech in v.13 aloud. What exactly does he plan, and who or what is missing from the plan?
2. Why does James answer a year-long business plan with a picture of a mist (v.14)? What is he saying about the difference between a year and a day?
3. What is the difference between saying "If the Lord wills" as a real confession and saying it as a habit? How would you tell the difference in your own speech?
4. Verse 17 says knowing the good and not doing it is sin. What is one good thing you already know you should do that you have been putting off — and what would the first small step be?
