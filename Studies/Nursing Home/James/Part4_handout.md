# James 2:1-13 - Do Not Show Partiality — Study Handout

## Verses

> **1** My brothers, show no partiality as you hold the faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory.
>
> **2** For if a man wearing a gold ring and fine clothing comes into your assembly, and a poor man in shabby clothing also comes in,
>
> **3** and if you pay attention to the one who wears the fine clothing and say, "You sit here in a good place," while you say to the poor man, "You stand over there," or, "Sit down at my feet,"
>
> **4** have you not then made distinctions among yourselves and become judges with evil thoughts?
>
> **5** Listen, my beloved brothers, has not God chosen those who are poor in the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom, which he has promised to those who love him?
>
> **6** But you have dishonored the poor man. Are not the rich the ones who oppress you, and the ones who drag you into court?
>
> **7** Are they not the ones who blaspheme the honorable name by which you were called?
>
> **8** If you really fulfill the royal law according to the Scripture, "You shall love your neighbor as yourself," you are doing well.
>
> **9** But if you show partiality, you are committing sin and are convicted by the law as transgressors.
>
> **10** For whoever keeps the whole law but fails in one point has become accountable for all of it.
>
> **11** For he who said, "Do not commit adultery," also said, "Do not murder." If you do not commit adultery but do murder, you have become a transgressor of the law.
>
> **12** So speak and so act as those who are to be judged under the law of liberty.
>
> **13** For judgment is without mercy to one who has shown no mercy. Mercy triumphs over judgment.
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

James — the Lord's half-brother and leader of the Jerusalem church — writes to Jewish believers scattered across the Roman world, a congregation so early it still calls its gathering a "synagogue" (v.2). Most of them were poor; the wealthy oppressed them and hauled them into court. Yet when a gold-ringed visitor arrived, the church fawned over him and told the poor man to sit on the floor. James stages that scene (vv.2-3), then dismantles it: favoritism turns the assembly into a corrupt courtroom (v.4), inverts God's choice of the poor (v.5), flatters the church's own oppressors (vv.6-7), breaks the royal law of neighbor-love (vv.8-11), and forgets that we will all stand before the Judge (vv.12-13). The passage leads straight into "faith without works is dead" (2:14-26) — how we treat the poor is the test case for whether faith acts.

**Big idea:** Faith in the Lord of glory cannot rank people by outward glory — God chose the poor, love is the royal law, and mercy triumphs over judgment.

## Info

### v.1
- **Partiality** (Greek: προσωπολημψία, *prosōpolēmpsia*) is literally "face-receiving" — the Hebrew courtroom idiom "to lift the face." Judges were forbidden it (Leviticus 19:15) because God shows none (Deuteronomy 10:17).
- "The Lord of glory": true glory belongs to Christ — against it, gold's glitter is counterfeit.

### v.2
- "Gold-ringed" (Greek: χρυσοδακτύλιος, *chrysodaktylios*) appears only here in the Greek Bible; the Roman gold ring was a badge of rank.
- "Assembly" is literally "synagogue" — the only NT use for a Christian meeting.
- "Fine" is literally "shining/bright" clothing vs. "filthy" — garments were the era's status broadcast.

### v.3
- First-century synagogues had a few tiered stone benches; honored guests sat, the rest stood or took the floor.
- "Sit down at my feet" is a posture of subjection. Assigning seats is rendering a verdict.

### v.4
- "Made distinctions" (Greek: διακρίνω, *diakrinō*) puns on "doubt/waver" (1:6) — the partial judge is a divided soul.
- The congregation has become a corrupt court, breaking Leviticus 19:15 in the hall meant to embody it.

### v.5
- God "chose" the poor — election language; "poor in the world" means poor by the world's reckoning.
- Echoes Jesus: "Blessed are you who are poor... yours is the kingdom" (Luke 6:20; cf. 1 Samuel 2:8).
- Faith and love for God — not poverty itself — mark the heirs.

### v.6
- "You have dishonored the poor man" — the church did the world's work.
- The rich "drag you into court": debt litigation skewed toward the wealthy. The church flatters the class destroying it.

### v.7
- "The honorable name by which you were called" — ownership idiom (Amos 9:12): Christ's name, invoked at baptism, slandered by the very people being honored.

### v.8
- The "royal law" is Leviticus 19:18 — from the same chapter that forbids partiality (19:15).
- Jesus ranked it second only to loving God (Mark 12:31).

### v.9
- Favoritism is not bad manners: "you are committing sin" — a "transgressor" is one who steps over the line.

### v.10
- The law is a whole; fail one point and you are "accountable for all." There is no ledger where kept commands offset broken ones.

### v.11
- One "he who said" stands behind both commands — defying one command defies the Lawgiver himself.

### v.12
- "So speak and so act" — words and deeds together, under the "law of liberty" (1:25): the law that frees rather than enslaves.

### v.13
- "Without mercy" (Greek: ἀνέλεος, *aneleos*) appears only here in the NT — and is overturned by the mercy that follows.
- "Triumphs" is literally "boasts/exults over": Mercy raises a victor's shout over Judgment (cf. Matthew 5:7; 18:32-35).

## Discussion

1. Walk through vv.2-3: what exactly does each visitor look like, and what exactly is said to each one?
2. James asks five questions in vv.4-7, each expecting "yes." Which one stings most, and why?
3. According to v.5, what has God done — and promised — for "those who are poor in the world"?
4. Why does James treat one act of favoritism as breaking the whole law (vv.9-11)?
5. What are our "gold rings" — the status markers that change how we treat people today?
6. "Mercy triumphs over judgment" (v.13): where do you need to show mercy this week, remembering the mercy you have received?
