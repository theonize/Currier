# Leader's Notes — James 2:14-26 - Faith Without Works Is Dead

## Verses

> **14** What good is it, my brothers, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? Can that faith save him?
>
> **15** If a brother or sister is poorly clothed and lacking in daily food,
>
> **16** and one of you says to them, "Go in peace, be warmed and filled," without giving them the things needed for the body, what good is that?
>
> **17** So also faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead.
>
> **18** But someone will say, "You have faith and I have works." Show me your faith apart from your works, and I will show you my faith by my works.
>
> **19** You believe that God is one; you do well. Even the demons believe—and shudder!
>
> **20** Do you want to be shown, you foolish person, that faith apart from works is useless?
>
> **21** Was not Abraham our father justified by works when he offered up his son Isaac on the altar?
>
> **22** You see that faith was active along with his works, and faith was completed by his works;
>
> **23** and the Scripture was fulfilled that says, "Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness"—and he was called a friend of God.
>
> **24** You see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone.
>
> **25** And in the same way was not also Rahab the prostitute justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out by another way?
>
> **26** For as the body apart from the spirit is dead, so also faith apart from works is dead.
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

**Setting**

- Written by **James**, the half-brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3; Galatians 1:19) and leader of the Jerusalem church — remembered by tradition as "James the Just," a man famed for practiced righteousness. Josephus records his death by stoning c. AD 62 (*Antiquities* 20.200).
- Probable date **c. AD 45-49**, possibly the earliest New Testament writing. If so, James is **not** rebutting Paul's letters (Galatians and Romans were not yet written) but engaging a slogan already circulating in Jewish-Christian circles.
- Audience: "the twelve tribes in the Dispersion" (James 1:1) — Jewish believers scattered outside Palestine, living near subsistence, proud of their monotheistic orthodoxy, and steeped in the Abraham story.
- James pastored a church that organized famine relief (Acts 11:29-30) and was charged above all to "remember the poor" (Galatians 2:10). The hungry brother of vv.15-16 was his daily reality, not a thought experiment.

**Genre and literary frame**

- **Paraenesis** (moral exhortation) in the form of a Greco-Roman **diatribe** — a staged debate with an imaginary opponent, complete with challenge question (v.14), objector (v.18), and insult (v.20).
- Second panel of the chapter-2 diptych: faith without **partiality** (2:1-13) / faith without **works** (2:14-26), stitched by the catchword *faith* and by the poorly-clothed figure who appears in both (2:2; v.15).
- Framed by an **inclusio**: "faith... is dead" (v.17) restated almost verbatim at v.26.

```
v.14      THESIS: "What good is it?" / "Can THAT faith save him?"
 vv.15-16   ILLUSTRATION: the naked, hungry brother; "be warmed and filled"
   v.17       REFRAIN: faith by itself is DEAD          ◄── inclusio top
 vv.18-19   DIATRIBE: "Show me..." / even demons believe — and SHUDDER
 v.20       APOSTROPHE: "O empty man" — faith is work-less (argē)
 vv.21-25   PROOFS: Abraham on Moriah (21-24) + Rahab at Jericho (25)
   v.26       REFRAIN: body without breath = faith without works = DEAD  ◄── bottom
```

**Big idea**

- The faith that saves is the faith that acts; a faith producing nothing is not weak faith but a corpse.

**Supporting movements**

- James is not attacking faith but a **species** of faith — the merely professed, creedal kind (the article in v.14 is anaphoric: "can *that* faith save him?").
- He proves it by the **lesser case**: demons hold flawless monotheism and are damned (v.19).
- He proves it by **two witnesses** — the Torah's rule (Deuteronomy 19:15) — deliberately chosen as opposites: the revered patriarch and a Gentile prostitute.
- The test is not doctrinal but diaconal: dead faith is exposed at the point where a real need meets a pious word.

---

## Topics

- **Dead faith vs. living faith** — bare assent is not saving faith; it is "dead" (vv.17, 26) and "useless" (v.20), and demons hold it too (v.19).
- **Mercy to the needy as the test** — James illustrates lifeless faith not with a doctrinal error but with a withheld meal (vv.15-16), binding justification to love of neighbor.
- **Faith completed by works** — works do not create faith; they bring it to its intended maturity, as at Moriah and Jericho (vv.21-25).
- **James and Paul** — same words (*faith*, *works*, *justify*), same proof-text (Genesis 15:6), opposite errors opposed (vv.21-24).

## Info

### v.14
- **"What good is it?"** (Greek: τί τὸ ὄφελος, *ti to ophelos*, G3786) brackets the whole illustration, sounding again at v.16.
- "someone **says** he has faith" — the accusative-infinitive distances the claim from the reality; a third-class conditional frames a recurring, representative case, not one incident.
- "Can **that** faith save him?" — the article (Greek: ἡ πίστις, *hē pistis*) is **anaphoric**, pointing back to the merely professed faith of the same verse. ESV/NASB "that," NIV "such"; the KJV's bare "faith" invites the misreading.
- The negative **μή** in the question expects the answer **"No."**
- **save** (Greek: σῴζω, *sōzō*, G4982) here looks to final, eschatological rescue — the judgment of 2:12-13.
- Catchword *faith* ties this verse back to 2:1, joining the two panels of the chapter.

### v.15
- The test case is a **brother or sister** (Greek: ἀδελφὸς ἢ ἀδελφή) — fictive-kinship language giving the destitute believer the binding claim of family. The explicit "sister" is rare and pointed.
- **poorly clothed** (Greek: γυμνοί, *gymnoi*, "naked," i.e. ill-clad) and **lacking daily food** (Greek: ἐφήμερος, *ephēmeros*, G2184, a NT hapax) — hand-to-mouth existence where one bad day means hunger.
- In a society with no public welfare, community almsgiving was the only mechanism keeping such a person alive.
- The image deliberately recalls the shabbily dressed poor man of 2:2 — the same failure of mercy, viewed from a new angle.

### v.16
- "**Go in peace, be warmed and filled**" is a recognizable farewell blessing (Hebrew: לֵךְ לְשָׁלוֹם, *lek lᵉšālôm*; cf. Judges 18:6; 1 Samuel 1:17; Mark 5:34) — here emptied of its substance.
- Three present imperatives in passive/middle form: the speaker tells the poor to *get themselves* warmed and fed while withholding the means.
- "the things needed for the **body**" — Torah and prophets commanded exactly this open-handedness (Deuteronomy 15:7-11; Isaiah 58:7, "share your bread with the hungry... cover the naked").
- The repeated "What good is it?" closes the bracket opened in v.14.

### v.17
- The verdict: faith **by itself** is **dead** (Greek: νεκρά, *nekra*, G3498).
- **καθ' ἑαυτήν** (*kath' heautēn*, "by itself") is decisive — such faith is dead not merely in its effects but intrinsically, left to itself.
- *nekros* denotes a **corpse**, not weakness: lifeless matter, intact in appearance and animated by nothing.
- This is the top of the inclusio closed at v.26.

### v.18
- "But someone will say" (Greek: ἀλλ' ἐρεῖ τις) summons the **diatribe** objector — stock form known from Epictetus and the Cynic-Stoic preachers.
- The most contested syntax in the letter: first-century manuscripts carried no quotation marks, so the objection is hard to assign.
- **δεῖξόν μοι** ("**show** me") your faith **apart from** (Greek: χωρίς, *chōris*, G5565) works — an impossibility. Faith is invisible; only *erga* render it demonstrable.
- Textual note: part of the Byzantine tradition reads ἐκ ("by") for χωρίς. The harder, better-attested χωρίς (ℵ A B C) is original, and sharper.

### v.19
- "God is one" cites the **Shema** (Deuteronomy 6:4) — recited morning and evening, bound in tefillin, fixed to the doorpost. It was the audience's badge of covenant identity.
- "you do well" (Greek: καλῶς ποιεῖς) redeploys the commendation of 2:8 with a sneer.
- **shudder** (Greek: φρίσσουσιν, *phrissousin*, G5425) — a NT hapax, near-onomatopoeic: to bristle, hair standing on end.
- The **reductio**: demons hold impeccable theology and are damned all the same. The empty professor ranks *beneath* a demon, since the demons at least tremble.
- The confession is materially attested — the Nash Papyrus and Qumran tefillin fragments preserve Deuteronomy 6:4.

### v.20
- **Apostrophe** to the absent fool: "**O empty man**" (Greek: ὦ ἄνθρωπε κενέ, *ō anthrōpe kene*) — the wisdom tradition's stock fool, hollow where substance should be. "Empty" echoes the empty words of v.16.
- **useless** (Greek: ἀργή, *argē*, G692) is a contraction of ἀ-εργός, "**not-working, work-less**" — a buried pun: faith without **works** (*erga*) is *a-erga*.
- Textual variant: the Byzantine majority (and the KJV) reads νεκρά, "dead," leveling the pun toward vv.17, 26. ἀργή is the harder and better-attested reading.

### v.21
- "**Abraham our father**" taps the audience's deepest paradigm; the **Aqedah** (Hebrew: עֲקֵדָה, *ʿăqēdâ*, "binding," Genesis 22) was Second Temple Judaism's supreme proof of tested faithfulness (Jubilees 17-18; 4 Maccabees).
- **justified** (Greek: ἐδικαιώθη, aorist passive of δικαιόω, G1344) — a completed past vindication.
- The question is framed with **οὐκ**, which (opposite to μή in v.14) expects the answer **"Yes."**
- **offered up** (Greek: ἀναφέρω, *anapherō*, G399) is sacrificial vocabulary; the altar stood in "the land of Moriah" (Genesis 22:2), identified in 2 Chronicles 3:1 with the temple mount — Jerusalem, where James likely wrote.

### v.22
- **συνήργει** (*synērgei*, G4903, imperfect) — faith "**was continually working with**" his works. The same *ergon* root that named the missing element now names the partnership.
- **ἐτελειώθη** (from τελειόω, *teleioō*, G5048, aorist passive) — faith "**was brought to completion**." Works are faith's *telos*, its designed destination, not a deficiency patched from outside.
- The grammar makes works the **maturation**, never the replacement, of faith.
- Joins the letter's "perfect/complete" motif (1:4, "let steadfastness have its full effect"; 1:25, "the perfect law").

### v.23
- "the Scripture **was fulfilled**" (Greek: ἐπληρώθη, *eplērōthē*) treats Genesis 22 as the ripening of Genesis 15:6, spoken decades earlier.
- **counted** (Greek: ἐλογίσθη, from λογίζομαι, G3049) is commercial-forensic — a sum credited to an account. James quotes the same LXX wording Paul quotes in Romans 4.
- "**friend of God**" (Greek: φίλος θεοῦ, *philos theou*) is a fixed honorific (Isaiah 41:8; 2 Chronicles 20:7; Jubilees 19:9), naming covenant intimacy **proven**, not merely professed.
- Crucial background: **1 Maccabees 2:52** already fused Genesis 15:6 with the testing of Genesis 22 — "found faithful when tested, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness." James stands inside a standard Jewish reading, not a polemic against Paul.

### v.24
- Shift to the **plural** "**you [all] see**" (Greek: ὁρᾶτε, *horate*) — James turns from the singular fool to the whole congregation for the verdict.
- **δικαιοῦται** is a **gnomic present** (a timeless rule), in contrast to the historical aorists of vv.21 and 25.
- **μόνον** ("alone") modifies the *kind* of faith in view — a solitary, work-less faith. This is the only place in Scripture where "faith" and "alone" stand together, and it is a negation.
- Paul's counter-statement (Romans 3:28) uses the same three words in different senses against the opposite error — see the Topics note and question 3 below.

### v.25
- "**in the same way**" (Greek: ὁμοίως, *homoiōs*) and the identical formula "Was not... justified by works?" bind Rahab to Abraham as the second of two witnesses.
- **Rahab the prostitute** (Greek: πόρνη, *pornē*, G4204 — etymologically "the one sold") is a Gentile, a woman, and a social outcast on every count. Paired with the revered patriarch, the two form a **merism** spanning the whole range of humanity.
- Her deed: she **received** (Greek: ὑποδέχομαι, *hypodechomai*, G5264) the **messengers** (Greek: ἀγγέλους, *angelous*, G32 — the scouts of Joshua 2) and sent them out another way (Joshua 2:15-16) — hospitality at mortal risk.
- Her faith is widely attested outside James: Matthew 1:5 (Messiah's genealogy), Hebrews 11:31, and 1 Clement 12 (c. AD 96), which celebrates her "faith and hospitality."

### v.26
- The closing simile is exact in form — **ὥσπερ... οὕτως** ("just as... so") — comparing body-without-**breath** (Greek: πνεῦμα, *pneuma*, G4151) to faith-without-works.
- Reaches back to Genesis 2:7, where God breathes life into the man: the image-bearer is a body-and-breath unity, and so is faith a trust-and-deed unity.
- Note the surprising mapping — **faith → body, works → animating breath**. The point is not which is higher but that the two are one living organism.
- The repeated νεκρά closes the inclusio opened at v.17. A body without breath is not a sleeping body but a **corpse**.

## Discussion

1. Walk slowly through vv.15-16. What exactly does the well-wisher say, and what does he withhold? Why do you think James illustrates dead faith with a withheld meal rather than with a doctrinal error?
   - **Leader's note:** Get the scene concrete before any theology — a real brother or sister, cold and hungry, sent off with a genuine blessing ("Go in peace" is a real farewell formula) that hands over no bread. The passive imperatives ("be warmed, be filled") push responsibility back onto the poor. Then surface why: chapter 2 opened by condemning partiality against the poor (2:1-13) and 1:27 defined pure religion as visiting orphans and widows. For James the reality of faith is exposed at the point of mercy, not the point of doctrine.

2. In v.19 James grants his opponent's orthodoxy — "You believe that God is one; you do well" — then adds, "Even the demons believe—and shudder." What kind of faith is James saying cannot save? Compare his wording back in v.14.
   - **Leader's note:** Push toward the article in v.14: "can *that* faith save him?" — the faith just described, the merely professed kind. James never denies that faith saves; he denies that *this* faith is the faith that saves. The demons are the limiting case: perfect theology, zero trust, terror instead of love. Land the sting — the audience's proudest possession was the Shema, recited twice daily, and James ranks a creed held without obedience beneath a demon's, since the demons at least tremble.

3. Verse 24 says a person is "justified by works and not by faith alone." Paul says a person is "justified by faith apart from works of the law" (Romans 3:28). Both quote Genesis 15:6. How do the two fit together?
   - **Leader's note:** Not a contradiction but two men fighting opposite errors with the same three words. Paul opposes **legalism** (works as the ground of acceptance); James opposes **dead orthodoxy** (a fruitless creed). *Justify* for Paul = God declares righteous; for James = the verdict is shown, vindicated in a life. *Works* for Paul = works of the law done for merit; for James = deeds of mercy that flow from trust. Key evidence: Abraham was reckoned righteous in Genesis 15:6, and that word was "fulfilled" (v.23) decades later at Moriah. If James is early (c. AD 45-49), he is not answering Paul at all. The Reformation maxim catches it: justified by faith alone, but not by a faith that remains alone.

4. "Show me your faith apart from your works" (v.18). If someone said that to you about this past month, what could you point to besides words? Where has a need you knew about received a blessing instead of the thing the body actually needed?
   - **Leader's note:** Keep this specific — a named person, a real need, this week. Two ditches to guard. Toward complacency: warm words offered as though they were sustenance. Toward panic: the tender conscience that starts producing works to *earn* acceptance. James intends neither; works **confirm** the life Christ gives, they never **found** it. A useful closing diagnostic from the passage: is my orthodoxy the orthodoxy of a worshiper, or of a demon who believes and only shudders?
