# James 5:13-20 - Prayer of faith — Study Handout

## Verses

> **13** Is anyone among you suffering? Let him pray. Is anyone cheerful? Let him sing praise.
>
> **14** Is anyone among you sick? Let him call for the elders of the church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord.
>
> **15** And the prayer of faith will save the one who is sick, and the Lord will raise him up. And if he has committed sins, he will be forgiven.
>
> **16** Therefore, confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person has great power as it is working.
>
> **17** Elijah was a man with a nature like ours, and he prayed fervently that it might not rain, and for three years and six months it did not rain on the earth.
>
> **18** Then he prayed again, and heaven gave rain, and the earth bore its fruit.
>
> **19** My brothers, if anyone among you wanders from the truth and someone brings him back,
>
> **20** let him know that whoever brings back a sinner from his wandering will save his soul from death and will cover a multitude of sins.
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

James ends his letter with prayer. He wrote to scattered believers who knew poverty, injustice, and sickness without modern medicine. Tradition remembers James himself as a man of such constant prayer that his knees grew callused.

In a few short lines he gives the church a response for every season of life. When we suffer, we pray. When we are glad, we sing. When we are sick, we call on the church to pray with us. When we have sinned, we confess and pray for one another. When someone drifts from the faith, we go after them.

James grounds all this in Elijah, a man “with a nature like ours,” whose prayers God answered. Powerful prayer is not only for spiritual heroes. It belongs to ordinary believers, in any chair or bed, who bring their needs and one another to the Lord.

**Big idea:** In every season of life God’s people turn to him together — praying, praising, confessing, and bringing back the wanderer — because the prayer of a righteous person has great power.

---

## Key Verse

> **16** Therefore, confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person has great power as it is working.

---

## Main Points

1. **Bring every season to God** (vv.13-14) — In suffering pray, in gladness sing praise, and in sickness call the elders to pray.

2. **Pray and confess together** (vv.15-16) — The Lord heals and forgives, so believers confess their sins to one another and pray for one another.

3. **Pray like Elijah** (vv.17-18) — Elijah was an ordinary man whose fervent prayer God answered, so ordinary believers may pray with confidence.

4. **Bring back the wanderer** (vv.19-20) — Whoever turns a straying believer back to the truth saves a soul from death.

---

## Info

### 1. Bring every season to God (vv.13-14)

#### v.13
- Hard times and happy times both belong to God. “Let him pray” means keep on praying, as a daily habit.
- “Sing praise” first meant to pluck strings, then to sing psalms. Gladness is meant to rise back to God as thanks.

#### v.14
- The sick person takes the first step: “let him call.” Asking for prayer takes humility; we need not suffer alone.
- Oil was the common medicine of the day. The prayer, offered “in the name of the Lord,” is the main action — not magic, but trust in him.

### 2. Pray and confess together (vv.15-16)

#### v.15
- “Save” can mean both heal and save, and “raise up” is also the word for resurrection. God cares for body and soul.
- James says “*if* he has committed sins.” Sickness is not automatically a sign of personal sin.
- This is a call to expectant prayer, not a promise that healing always comes now. God sometimes heals the body in this life and always heals it finally in the resurrection.

#### v.16
- We confess to God, and also to trusted believers who will pray for us. Honest confession brings hidden sin into the light.
- “Has great power as it is working” — prayer really works, because God has chosen to act through it.

### 3. Pray like Elijah (vv.17-18)

#### v.17
- Elijah had “a nature like ours” — the same frailty and feelings. His power came from the God who answered him.
- “Prayed fervently” is literally “with prayer he prayed”: earnest, wholehearted prayer (1 Kings 17-18).

#### v.18
- He prayed again, and rain returned. God used one man’s prayer to end a long season of judgment.
- If God heard Elijah, he hears us. Prayer offered from a bed or a chair is real ministry.

### 4. Bring back the wanderer (vv.19-20)

#### v.19
- “Wanders” pictures a sheep straying from the path; compare the shepherd seeking the lost sheep in Luke 15:3-7.
- “Brings back” is the Bible’s word for turning or repentance. God uses people to call others home.

#### v.20
- Turning someone back saves a soul from death — nothing could matter more.
- “Cover a multitude of sins” echoes Proverbs 10:12, “love covers all offenses.” Covering means forgiveness (Psalm 32:1).
- The letter ends here, handing each of us the role of restorer. A prayer, a note, a call, or a visit can begin it.

## Discussion

1. What three situations does James name in vv.13-14, and what response does he give for each?

2. Why do you think James joins healing, forgiveness, and confession together in vv.15-16, and what does the small word “if” in v.15 guard against?

3. Why does James stress that Elijah was “a man with a nature like ours”? What does that mean for our own prayers?

4. Who could you pray for by name this week — someone sick, someone struggling, or someone who has drifted from the faith — and how might you reach out to them?
