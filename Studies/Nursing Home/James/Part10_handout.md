# James 5:1-6 - Warning to the Rich — Study Handout

## Verses

> **1** Come now, you rich, weep and howl for the miseries that are coming upon you.
>
> **2** Your riches have rotted and your garments are moth-eaten.
>
> **3** Your gold and silver have corroded, and their corrosion will be evidence against you and will eat your flesh like fire. You have laid up treasure in the last days.
>
> **4** Behold, the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you kept back by fraud, are crying out against you, and the cries of the harvesters have reached the ears of the Lord of hosts.
>
> **5** You have lived on the earth in luxury and in self-indulgence. You have fattened your hearts in a day of slaughter.
>
> **6** You have condemned and murdered the righteous person. He does not resist you.
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

James has just rebuked the merchant who plans a year without God (4:13-17). Now, with a second "Come now," he turns on a harder target: the rich landowner whose estate runs on day-labourers he does not pay. The tone changes completely. This is not wisdom counsel but a prophet's woe, in the voice of Amos and Isaiah. The rich are told to weep and howl, never to repent, because the verdict is already in. They are addressed as if absent; the real listeners are the poor believers who overhear, and who are meant to take comfort. Four charges climb from property to life: they hoard, they defraud, they indulge, they kill. At the centre stands the unpaid wage, which has a voice the Lord of hosts has already heard. James does not condemn wealth as such, only wealth hoarded, stolen, indulged, and weaponized.

**Big idea:** The Lord of hosts hears the cry of the defrauded and has already entered judgment on wealth that is hoarded, stolen, indulged, and weaponized — so the oppressed may wait in patience, and the comfortable must examine what their money is for.

## Key Verse

> **4** Behold, the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you kept back by fraud, are crying out against you, and the cries of the harvesters have reached the ears of the Lord of hosts.

---

## Main Points

1. **Weep and Howl** (v.1) — The rich are summoned not to repent but to wail, because their miseries are already on the way.
2. **The Hoard That Testifies** (vv.2-3) — Every form of stored wealth has already rotted, and the corrosion itself is evidence that will consume its owner.
3. **The Wage That Cries Out** (v.4) — The withheld wage has a voice, and the Lord of hosts has already heard it.
4. **Fattened for Slaughter** (vv.5-6) — Luxury is cattle gorging on slaughter-day, and crushing the unresisting righteous draws the opposition of the God who opposes the proud.

---

## Info

### 1. Weep and Howl (v.1)

#### v.1
- **"Come now"** (Greek: ἄγε νῦν, *age nyn*) — the same prophet's bark as 4:13. Two matched oracles: the merchant who forgot God, the landlord who defies him.
- **"You rich"** — not the wealthy in general but the exploiting class of 2:6 who "drag you into court." Spoken over them, for the congregation's hearing.
- **"Howl"** (Greek: ὀλολύζω, *ololyzō*) — the prophets' shriek over doomed nations (Isaiah 13:6; Amos 8:3). A funeral dirge, not a call to repent (cf. Luke 6:24).

### 2. The Hoard That Testifies (vv.2-3)

#### v.2
- Ancient wealth came in three forms: **produce** that rots, **garments** the moth eats, **gold and silver** that corrode (v.3). James names all three so nothing escapes.
- The verbs are past-tense-complete: the goods still look fine, but heaven's ledger already reads "rotted."
- Behind it stands Jesus: "Do not lay up treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy" (Matthew 6:19-20).

#### v.3
- Gold does not rust. The impossibility is the point: even "incorruptible" wealth is under sentence.
- **"Corrosion"** (Greek: ἰός, *ios*) means both "rust" and "venom" — the same word as the tongue's "deadly poison" (3:8). The tarnish turns on the hoarder.
- **"Evidence against you"** is courtroom language. The idle hoard takes the witness stand.
- **"In the last days"** — the worst season to stockpile what decays (cf. Luke 12:16-21). They thought they stored wealth; they stored wrath (Romans 2:5).

### 3. The Wage That Cries Out (v.4)

#### v.4
- **"Behold"** — the prophet points to the unheard plaintiffs. This verse is the centre of the passage.
- Day-labourers were paid each evening; no wage, no supper. The Law commanded same-day pay "lest he cry against you to the LORD" (Deuteronomy 24:14-15; Leviticus 19:13).
- **The wage itself "cries out"** — unpaid coin is given a voice, joined by the harvesters' cries: two witnesses. It joins Abel's blood (Genesis 4:10), Israel's groan in Egypt (Exodus 2:23), and the martyrs' "How long?" (Revelation 6:9-10).
- **"Have reached"** — already lodged in God's hearing.
- **"Lord of hosts"** (Greek: κύριος σαβαώθ, *kyrios Sabaōth*; Hebrew: יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, "LORD of armies") — left untranslated; a near-quote of Isaiah 5:9. Against the landlord's hired hands stands the commander of heaven's armies.

### 4. Fattened for Slaughter (vv.5-6)

#### v.5
- **"Luxury... self-indulgence"** — words for a life gone soft; the second is used of the widow "dead even while she lives" (1 Timothy 5:6).
- **"Fattened your hearts"** is livestock language; a "fat" heart is a numbed conscience (Isaiah 6:10).
- **"In a day of slaughter"** (Jeremiah 12:3) — the cattle gorge on the very day they are fattened for. The reapers of v.4 become the reaped.

#### v.6
- **"Condemned... murdered"** — no "and" in the Greek; rigged courts and killing fused into one act (cf. 2:6).
- **"The righteous person"** — the defenceless godly poor, and behind them the Righteous One who "opened not his mouth" (Isaiah 53:7; Acts 3:14; 1 Peter 2:23). James "the Just" would himself die this way.
- **"He does not resist"** (Greek: ἀντιτάσσεται, *antitassetai*) — the exact verb of 4:6, "God **opposes** the proud." The victim does not oppose the rich; God does.
- The oracle ends on the victim's helplessness — the posture James then commends to the church: "Be patient... the Judge is standing at the door" (5:7-9).

## Discussion

1. Read v.1 again. James tells the rich to "weep and howl" but never tells them to repent. Who is really meant to hear this speech, and what is it meant to do for them?
2. In vv.2-3 James says the gold has "corroded" and the corrosion is "evidence against you." Gold does not rust. What is he saying about hoarded wealth, and about what it will one day say about us?
3. Verse 4 says the unpaid wages "are crying out" and have "reached the ears of the Lord of hosts." What does it mean that God hears a cry no human court will hear, and where else in the Bible does that happen?
4. James says the rich have "fattened their hearts" (v.5) — grown numb through comfort — and that the righteous one "does not resist" (v.6). Where in your own life could comfort be dulling your conscience, and how does trusting the Judge (5:7-9) change how you respond to being wronged?
