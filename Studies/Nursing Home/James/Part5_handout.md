# James 2:14-26 - Faith Without Works Is Dead — Study Handout

## Verses

> **14** What good is it, my brothers, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? Can that faith save him?
>
> **15** If a brother or sister is poorly clothed and lacking in daily food,
>
> **16** and one of you says to them, "Go in peace, be warmed and filled," without giving them the things needed for the body, what good is that?
>
> **17** So also faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead.
>
> **18** But someone will say, "You have faith and I have works." Show me your faith apart from your works, and I will show you my faith by my works.
>
> **19** You believe that God is one; you do well. Even the demons believe—and shudder!
>
> **20** Do you want to be shown, you foolish person, that faith apart from works is useless?
>
> **21** Was not Abraham our father justified by works when he offered up his son Isaac on the altar?
>
> **22** You see that faith was active along with his works, and faith was completed by his works;
>
> **23** and the Scripture was fulfilled that says, "Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness"—and he was called a friend of God.
>
> **24** You see that a person is justified by works and not by faith alone.
>
> **25** And in the same way was not also Rahab the prostitute justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out by another way?
>
> **26** For as the body apart from the spirit is dead, so also faith apart from works is dead.
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

James — the half-brother of Jesus and leader of the Jerusalem church — writes around AD 45-49 to Jewish believers scattered across the Roman world. They were poor, surrounded by the poor, and proud of their orthodoxy. This is the second half of a two-panel chapter: faith without partiality (2:1-13), then faith without works (2:14-26). The style is a staged debate, with an imagined opponent who objects (v.18) and gets called a fool (v.20).

James's target is not faith but a counterfeit of it — a faith that only *says*. He tests it three ways: a hungry brother sent away with a blessing (vv.15-16), demons who hold flawless theology and only shudder (v.19), and a corpse (v.26). Against these he sets two witnesses chosen as opposites, the revered patriarch and a Gentile prostitute, both vindicated by what they did.

**Big idea:** The faith that saves is the faith that acts; a faith producing nothing is not weak faith but a corpse.

---

## Topics

- **Dead faith vs. living faith** — bare assent is "dead" (vv.17, 26) and "useless" (v.20); demons hold it too (v.19).
- **Mercy as the test** — James illustrates lifeless faith with a withheld meal, not a doctrinal error (vv.15-16).
- **Faith completed by works** — works do not create faith; they bring it to maturity (vv.21-25).
- **James and Paul** — same words, same proof-text, opposite errors opposed (vv.21-24).

---

## Info

### v.14
- "What good is it?" (Greek: τί τὸ ὄφελος, *ti to ophelos*) brackets the illustration, sounding again in v.16.
- Someone *says* he has faith — the claim is held at arm's length from the reality.
- "Can **that** faith save him?" The Greek article points back to the merely professed faith just named, and the question expects the answer *no*.

### vv.15-17
- A **brother or sister** — family language that gives the destitute believer a binding claim on the church. The explicit "sister" is rare and pointed.
- "Go in peace, be warmed and filled" is a real farewell blessing, here emptied of substance; the verb forms tell the poor to get *themselves* warmed and fed.
- In a world without public welfare, the community's giving was what kept the destitute alive (cf. Isaiah 58:7).
- The verdict: faith **by itself** is **dead** (Greek: νεκρά, *nekra*, G3498) — not weak, but a corpse.

### vv.18-20
- The objector is a debating device. "Show me your faith **apart from** your works" is an impossibility — faith is invisible; deeds are what make it visible.
- "God is one" is the **Shema** (Deuteronomy 6:4), recited twice daily, the badge of Jewish identity. James grants it, then ranks it beneath a demon's creed — the demons at least **shudder** (Greek: φρίσσουσιν, *phrissousin*, G5425).
- "O foolish person" is the wisdom tradition's stock fool, hollow where substance should be.
- "Useless" (Greek: ἀργή, *argē*, G692) is a pun: the word is literally "**work-less**" — faith without *works* is *work-less*.

### vv.21-24
- **Abraham** offering Isaac (Genesis 22) was, for this audience, the supreme proof of tested faithfulness.
- Faith "was active along with" his works and "was **completed**" by them (Greek: ἐτελειώθη, G5048) — works are faith's intended destination, not a patch on a defect.
- Genesis 15:6 was "**fulfilled**" decades later at the altar; the honorific "friend of God" (Isaiah 41:8; 2 Chronicles 20:7) names intimacy proven, not professed.
- "Not by faith **alone**" (v.24) is the only place in Scripture where *faith* and *alone* stand together — and it describes a solitary, work-less faith.

### v.25
- **Rahab** — Gentile, woman, prostitute — is the deliberate opposite of Abraham. Together the two span the whole range of humanity.
- Her deed was risky hospitality: she welcomed the spies and sent them out another way (Joshua 2). The rest of Scripture agrees with James's verdict (Matthew 1:5; Hebrews 11:31).

### v.26
- The closing image reaches back to Genesis 2:7, where God breathes life into the man.
- Note the mapping: faith is the **body**, works the **breath**. Remove the breath and what remains is not a sleeping body but a corpse.

## Discussion

1. Walk slowly through vv.15-16. What exactly does the well-wisher say, and what does he withhold? Why do you think James illustrates dead faith with a withheld meal rather than with a doctrinal error?
2. In v.19 James grants his opponent's orthodoxy — "You believe that God is one; you do well" — then adds, "Even the demons believe—and shudder." What kind of faith is James saying cannot save? Compare his wording back in v.14.
3. Verse 24 says a person is "justified by works and not by faith alone." Paul says a person is "justified by faith apart from works of the law" (Romans 3:28). Both quote Genesis 15:6. How do the two fit together?
4. "Show me your faith apart from your works" (v.18). If someone said that to you about this past month, what could you point to besides words? Where has a need you knew about received a blessing instead of the thing the body actually needed?
