# James 3:13-18 - Wisdom from Above — Study Handout

## Verses

> **13** Who is wise and understanding among you? By his good conduct let him show his works in the meekness of wisdom.
>
> **14** But if you have bitter jealousy and selfish ambition in your hearts, do not boast and be false to the truth.
>
> **15** This is not the wisdom that comes down from above, but is earthly, unspiritual, demonic.
>
> **16** For where jealousy and selfish ambition exist, there will be disorder and every vile practice.
>
> **17** But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial and sincere.
>
> **18** And a harvest of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace.
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

James — the half-brother of Jesus and leader of the Jerusalem church — has just finished his anatomy of the tongue (3:1-12). Now he names the disease behind the symptom: which *wisdom* is driving the mouth? His scattered congregations were competing for the teacher's honored seat, and the rivalry was tearing at the assembly. So James sets two wisdoms side by side, in the old "two-ways" manner of Psalm 1 and Proverbs: one comes down from God and shows itself in meek, peaceable conduct; the other — clever, envious, ambitious — rises from below and is known by the chaos it leaves behind. The test is never the claim but the fruit. He closes with a farmer's proverb: peace is a seed, and righteousness is its harvest.

**Big idea:** True wisdom comes down from God and proves itself in meek, peaceable conduct; the counterfeit is exposed by the disorder it breeds, while peacemakers reap a harvest of righteousness.

---

## Topics

- **Two wisdoms** — every life runs on a wisdom from above or from below, and each is known by its fruit (vv.13-18).
- **Wisdom shown in meekness** — the proof of wisdom is conduct and controlled strength, not cleverness or winning arguments (v.13).
- **Jealousy and selfish ambition** — the twin heart-vices behind community conflict, breeding disorder and every vile practice (vv.14-16).
- **The peacemaker's harvest** — righteousness grows like a crop, sown in peace by those who make peace (vv.17-18).

---

## Info

### v.13
- "Wise and understanding" echoes Moses' search for leaders (Deuteronomy 1:13) — this is a leadership question, following "not many of you should become teachers" (3:1).
- Wisdom is *shown*, not claimed — by good conduct, a settled way of life; it mirrors 2:18, "I will show you my faith by my works."
- "**Meekness** of wisdom" (Greek: πραΰτης, *praÿtēs*) — not weakness but controlled strength: the virtue of Moses (Numbers 12:3) and of Jesus, "gentle and lowly in heart" (Matthew 11:29).

### v.14
- "**Bitter** jealousy" — the same word as the "bitter" water of 3:11: James has found the polluted spring, and it is the jealous heart.
- "**Selfish ambition**" (Greek: ἐριθεία, *eritheia*) — a political word: the mercenary canvassing of partisans for office. Faction-spirit dressed up as insight.
- Boasting while envious is itself "false to the truth" — the claim of wisdom refutes itself.

### v.15
- The counterfeit's pedigree descends step by step: **earthly** → **unspiritual** (the merely natural soul, without the Spirit) → **demonic**.
- "Demonic" (Greek: δαιμονιώδης, *daimoniōdēs*) appears only here in the New Testament — the same infernal source as the tongue "set on fire by hell" (3:6).

### v.16
- A compressed proverb: where jealousy and ambition live, disorder and every vile practice follow — no exceptions.
- "**Disorder**" is the word for political instability, even anarchy; it is cousin to the "restless" tongue (3:8) and the "unstable" double-minded man (1:8).

### v.17
- Wisdom from above is "**first pure**" — purity of motive is the root; the other seven marks grow from it: peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial, sincere.
- "Full of mercy" reverses the tongue "full of deadly poison" (3:8); "sincere" means literally *without a mask*.
- The list reads like the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23) — and like a portrait of Jesus himself. This wisdom is asked for and given (1:5), not manufactured.

### v.18
- A farmer's proverb: peace is the seed and the soil; righteousness is the harvest (Isaiah 32:17; Hosea 10:12).
- "Those who make peace" stands one Greek word from Jesus' beatitude: "Blessed are the peacemakers" (Matthew 5:9). The truly wise are recognizable — they are the peace-sowers.

---

## Discussion

1. Look at v.13. How does James say real wisdom is proven — and what surprises you about the word "meekness"?
2. In v.15 James calls jealousy-driven cleverness "earthly, unspiritual, demonic." Why such strong language for attitudes we often treat as normal?
3. Read the eight marks of v.17 slowly. Which one do you find hardest — and what would it look like in an ordinary week?
4. Verse 18 pictures peace as seed sown now and righteousness as the harvest later. Where is God asking you to sow peace this week — and what makes that sowing costly?
