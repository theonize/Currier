# James 4:1-12 - Warning Against Worldliness — Study Handout

## Verses

> **1** What causes quarrels and what causes fights among you? Is it not this, that your passions are at war within you?
>
> **2** You desire and do not have, so you murder. You covet and cannot obtain, so you fight and quarrel. You do not have, because you do not ask.
>
> **3** You ask and do not receive, because you ask wrongly, to spend it on your passions.
>
> **4** You adulterous people! Do you not know that friendship with the world is enmity with God? Therefore whoever wishes to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God.
>
> **5** Or do you suppose it is to no purpose that the Scripture says, "He yearns jealously over the spirit that he has made to dwell in us"?
>
> **6** But he gives more grace. Therefore it says, "God opposes the proud but gives grace to the humble."
>
> **7** Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.
>
> **8** Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, you sinners, and purify your hearts, you double-minded.
>
> **9** Be wretched and mourn and weep. Let your laughter be turned to mourning and your joy to gloom.
>
> **10** Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you.
>
> **11** Do not speak evil against one another, brothers. The one who speaks against a brother or judges his brother, speaks evil against the law and judges the law. But if you judge the law, you are not a doer of the law but a judge.
>
> **12** There is only one lawgiver and judge, he who is able to save and to destroy. But who are you to judge your neighbor?
>
> — *English Standard Version (ESV)*

---

## Overview

James has just shown that jealousy and selfish ambition breed "disorder" (3:16). Now the disorder is in the open: scattered Jewish-Christian congregations, squeezed by famine and a homeland drifting toward war, are fighting one another over honor, goods, and status. James refuses to treat the symptom without naming the disease. Like a prophet arraigning an unfaithful bride, he traces the quarrels to cravings at war inside each believer and calls their flirtation with the world *adultery* against a jealous Husband — then pivots on four words: "But he gives more grace." Ten rapid commands follow, each welded to a promise. A closing test case — slander — shows pride climbing onto God's judgment seat.

**Big idea:** Our quarrels grow from cravings that have become gods and from a heart flirting with the world; the cure is not winning the argument but humbling ourselves before a jealous, gracious God who draws near to those who draw near — and stepping down from the judgment seat that is his alone.

---

## Topics

- **The war within** — quarrels spring from unsatisfied cravings inside us, and from prayer that is absent or self-serving (vv.1-3).
- **Friendship with the world** — courting the world's values is spiritual adultery against a God who yearns jealously over his people (vv.4-5).
- **Grace to the humble** — God opposes the proud but gives greater grace to those who submit, draw near, and grieve their sin (vv.6-10).
- **One Lawgiver and Judge** — to slander or judge a brother is to sit on a bench that belongs to God alone (vv.11-12).

---

## Info

### v.1
- "Quarrels and fights" are literally *wars and battles*; James locates the front line *inside*.
- "**Passions**" (Greek: ἡδοναί, *hēdonai*) — root of "hedonism"; craving pictured as troops on campaign "in your members."

### v.2
- A staccato chain: desire → lack → murder; covet → fail → fight. "You murder" is hyperbole in the manner of Jesus (Matthew 5:21-22) — hatred is already murder. "Because you do not **ask**" reverses 1:5.

### v.3
- The fault is motive, not technique: prayer as a procurement service for cravings.

### v.4
- "**You adulterous people!**" (Greek: μοιχαλίδες, *moichalides*) — feminine, "adulteresses": the congregation is God's unfaithful bride (Hosea 1-3; Jeremiah 3:20).
- "**Friendship**" (Greek: φιλία, *philia*) was a sworn alliance — a friend of the world has taken a rival lover (Matthew 6:24; 1 John 2:15-17).

### v.5
- No OT text matches; James distills the theme of the **jealous God** (Exodus 20:5; 34:14, "whose name is Jealous") — not insecurity but the exclusive love of a faithful Husband who will not share his bride.

### v.6
- "But he gives **more grace**" — the hinge; grace *greater* than the world's pull and the war within.
- Quotes Proverbs 3:34 (so does 1 Peter 5:5). "**Opposes**" is military: God draws up battle lines against the proud.

### v.7
- First of **ten commands** (vv.7-10). "**Submit**" (Greek: ὑποτάγητε, *hypotagēte*) — fall into rank under God.
- "Resist the **devil**" (literally *the slanderer*) — and he will flee. Surrender comes before resistance (1 Peter 5:8-9).

### v.8
- "**Draw near**... he will draw near" — the center of the call (Zechariah 1:3).
- "Cleanse **hands**... purify **hearts**" — deeds and motives (Psalm 24:4). "**Double-minded**" (Greek: δίψυχοι, *dipsychoi*, "two-souled") — James's word from 1:8: two friendships, two souls.

### v.9
- A liturgy of repentance in Joel's cadence (Joel 2:12-13). Not a joyless faith (1:2 commands joy) — grief that refuses to laugh off sin (Matthew 5:4).

### v.10
- "**Humble yourselves**... he will **exalt** you" — the lifting is God's alone; the way up runs down (Luke 14:11; Philippians 2:8-9).

### v.11
- "**Speak evil against**" (Greek: καταλαλέω, *katalaleō*) — slander (Leviticus 19:16). To slander a brother is to judge him, and so to judge the law of neighbor-love (2:8) — resigning from *doing* it to sit as its critic.

### v.12
- "Only **one** lawgiver and judge" — the Shema's "one" (Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 33:22). "**Lawgiver**" (Greek: νομοθέτης, *nomothetēs*) occurs nowhere else in the New Testament.
- "Who are **you**?" — "Neighbor" echoes Leviticus 19:18: the slanderer judges the very neighbor he is told to love.

## Discussion

1. Read vv.1-3. Where does James say our quarrels really come from — and what does he say has gone wrong with our praying?
2. James calls friendship with the world "adultery" (v.4) and says God "yearns jealously" over us (v.5). Why marriage language — and is God's jealousy good news or bad news?
3. Look at the ten commands in vv.7-10 and the promises attached to them ("he will flee," "he will draw near," "he will exalt you"). Which of these feels most urgent for you right now?
4. Verses 11-12 say that when we speak against or judge a fellow believer we are sitting in God's seat. Where are you most tempted to do that — and what would stepping down look like this week?
